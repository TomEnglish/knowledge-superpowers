# Thyroid hormone treatment and hard outcomes: heart events, deaths and the harms of over-replacement

## Question
What does giving thyroid hormone do to heart events and deaths, and not only to laboratory numbers? Covered: (a) overt hypothyroidism, (b) subclinical hypothyroidism, (c) people with hypothyroid symptoms and a normal TSH, (d) trials and older studies that used thyroid hormone, dextrothyroxine or thyroid-mimicking drugs as a cholesterol or heart treatment, and (e) the harms of over-replacement, by degree of TSH lowering.

## Key Findings

### The size of the evidence base
- Randomised evidence on heart events and deaths is very small. In subclinical hypothyroidism it amounts to 842 older people with 44 cardiovascular events and 21 deaths, plus 95 people after a heart attack [04.5][04.6]. In overt hypothyroidism, in adults under 65 with subclinical hypothyroidism, and in people with symptoms and a normal TSH, I found no placebo-controlled trial that counted heart events or deaths (searches listed under Gaps).
- Everything else is observational (treated against untreated people, or outcomes by TSH level on treatment) or uncontrolled historical series, and is confounded by who gets treated and who takes the tablets.

### (a) Overt hypothyroidism
- I found no trial that randomised overt hypothyroidism to treatment or no treatment, and withholding treatment would be unethical. The benefit is inferred from natural history and cohorts.
- Danish register (235,168 people with a TSH result, 1995 to 2011, median 7.2 years): untreated hypothyroid people (n = 673) had higher mortality than euthyroid controls (HR 1.46, 95% CI 1.26 to 1.69); treated hypothyroid people did not [04.50].
- Tayside, Scotland (15,889 treated primary hypothyroid patients, 1994 to 2001): no excess all-cause mortality or serious vascular events against the general population, but more non-fatal ischaemic heart disease (SIR 1.23, 1.10 to 1.36) and dysrhythmia (SIR 1.32, 1.11 to 1.57) [04.65].
- Korea (501,882 levothyroxine-treated patients, mean 6 years): mortality stayed slightly raised against matched controls (HR 1.14, 1.12 to 1.16), highest in the first year [04.67]. In Danish twins the excess mortality of diagnosed hypothyroidism (HR 1.52) disappeared within identical-twin pairs (HR 1.06, 0.55 to 2.05), which points to shared-background confounding [04.66].
- Among people already on treatment, an under-treated TSH carries the largest risks in every dataset: TSH above 10 mIU/L, death HR 2.21 (2.07 to 2.36), heart failure 1.42, ischaemic heart disease 1.18 [04.48]; cardiovascular death HR 2.13 at TSH 10 to 20 and 2.67 above 20 [04.49]. High TSH on treatment also marks missed tablets and illness, so these figures overstate what dosing alone does.
- Starting replacement can unmask coronary disease. In 51 patients with hypothyroidism and angina, control of hypothyroidism was unsatisfactory in two fifths and control of angina in one third [04.38]. The US label tells prescribers to start below the full dose in the elderly and in heart disease because of cardiac adverse reactions including atrial fibrillation (label revised 2/2024, checked 21 November 2026) [04.64]. Danish heart-failure patients on levothyroxine had higher mortality (IRR 1.25 for ongoing and 1.13 for new treatment), a comparison confounded by indication [04.68].

- TRUST was designed for 3,000 participants with vascular events as a co-primary outcome. Recruitment was cut to 737 for several reasons, chiefly delays in procuring levothyroxine and matching placebo, and vascular events were demoted to a secondary outcome [04.3][04.1]. The investigators' final report says the trial "was underpowered to detect any impact of levothyroxine on cardiovascular events or mortality" and "cannot exclude" protection or harm [04.1].
- My calculation (Schoenfeld approximation, 80% power, two-sided 5%): showing a 25% reduction needs about 380 events. The 44 pooled events could detect only a hazard ratio near 0.43 or lower. The trials therefore say nothing either way about a moderate effect on heart events or deaths.
- TRUST enrolled mild, largely symptom-free disease. Of 2,647 people re-tested after an earlier raised TSH, 62% had reverted below 4.60. The report states there were "few participants with baseline TSH >10" and that "symptom levels at study entry were low" [04.1]. Its results do not cover younger adults, a TSH above 10, or marked symptoms.
- In the TRUST participants with the highest symptom burden (132 with a symptom score above 30), symptoms improved by 12.3 points on levothyroxine and 10.4 on placebo (difference −2.0, −5.5 to 1.5) [04.17].
- The 2018 meta-analysis (21 trials, 2,192 adults, mean ages 32 to 74, mean baseline TSH 4.4 to 12.8, duration 3 to 18 months) found only TRUST reported cardiovascular events or deaths and graded that evidence low [04.7]. The 2019 BMJ panel's strong recommendation against treatment excluded TSH above 20, said it may not apply to severe symptoms or adults of 30 or under, and noted that "harms were measured in only one trial with few events" [04.8].

### (b) Subclinical hypothyroidism: observational comparisons (all confounded by who is treated)
- UK, new TSH 5.01 to 10.0, median 7.6 years: treated adults aged 40 to 70 had fewer ischaemic heart events (68 of 1,634 vs 97 of 1,459; HR 0.61, 0.39 to 0.95); over 70 there was no difference (HR 0.99; 0.59 to 1.33 as printed) [04.9].
- Denmark, 12,212 adults, median 5.0 years: no association with heart attack (IRR 1.08), cardiovascular death (1.02) or death (1.03, 0.90 to 1.19), except lower mortality under 65 (IRR 0.63, 0.40 to 0.99) [04.10]. In 1,192 Danish patients with heart disease, no benefit or harm (death IRR 1.17, 0.90 to 1.52) [04.11].
- Meta-analysis of 5 observational studies and 2 trials (21,055 adults): no association overall (death RR 0.95, 0.75 to 1.22); under 65 to 70, lower all-cause (RR 0.50, 0.29 to 0.85) and cardiovascular death (RR 0.54, 0.37 to 0.80) [04.12].
- UK CPRD, 76,946 matched pairs, TSH 5 to 10, median 1.6 years: slightly fewer major cardiovascular events (HR 0.88, 0.83 to 0.93) [04.13].
- The same UK team analysed the same database twice. The 2025 cohort (53,899 adults over 50) reported fewer cardiovascular events (HR 0.91) but more bone events (HR 1.21) and higher mortality (HR 1.17, 1.13 to 1.22) [04.14]. The 2026 emulated target trial (22,621) reported fewer cardiovascular events (HR 0.82), no bone harm (HR 1.04) and lower mortality (HR 0.71, 0.67 to 0.75) [04.15]. The direction of the mortality result depends on the analytic method.
- Untreated risk rises only at higher TSH: coronary events HR 1.00 at TSH 4.5 to 6.9, 1.17 (0.96 to 1.43) at 7.0 to 9.9 and 1.89 (1.28 to 2.80) at 10 to 19.9; total mortality not raised (55,287 participants, 11 cohorts) [04.16].
- Over-treatment of mild disease is common. In a Dutch study of 370 adults aged 60 or over on stable levothyroxine, 25.7% stopped it and kept adequate thyroid function at 1 year (63.6% of the 88 on 50 µg or less), with no relevant change in quality of life [04.18].

### (c) Hypothyroid symptoms with a normal TSH
- I found no study of any design that counted heart events or deaths in people treated for symptoms with normal tests. The evidence is on symptoms only.
- UK general hospital clinic, BMJ 2001, double-blind crossover: 25 patients (22 completed) with at least three of six symptoms for six months and tests in the reference range; levothyroxine 100 µg fixed dose for 12 weeks vs placebo. TSH fell to 0.66 vs 1.77 mU/L. No difference in wellbeing or cognition; patients improved markedly on placebo. No power calculation; funded by a clinical biochemistry scholarship [04.19]. It did not test dose titration to symptom relief, long treatment or desiccated thyroid.
- The question was tested before the TSH era. St Louis 1960, double-blind: 18 patients with a basal metabolic rate of −15% or lower (mean −21%), normal protein-bound iodine and normal radioiodine uptake, given thyroxine 0.1 to 0.3 mg, triiodothyronine 0.025 to 0.075 mg or lactose for 4 weeks, with blind switches in 8. No significant difference in metabolic rate or symptoms; some patients did best on lactose [04.20]. Minneapolis 1960, double-blind, 20 cases: as many improved on placebo as on triiodothyronine [04.21].
- An uncontrolled UK series reports 139 clinically hypothyroid, biochemically euthyroid patients titrated from 25 µg upward; 76% improved in more than 12 of 16 features. I saw only a search summary of the abstract, so this is a lead; the authors themselves asked for a placebo-controlled trial [04.22].
- In people already treated for hypothyroidism, blinded dose changes that moved TSH across and near the reference range (0.3, 1.0 and 2.8 mU/L in 56 patients; 1.85, 3.93 and 9.49 in 138) did not change symptoms, wellbeing or cognition. Patients preferred the dose they believed was higher [04.23][04.24].
- Stated at full strength for each side. Against: every blinded test so far shows a large placebo response and no hormone effect. For: no trial has tested the historical method (titrate to relief over months, often with desiccated thyroid), the trials total about 60 symptom-defined patients, and the main comparisons ran 4 to 12 weeks (one patient in a 1960 study stayed on thyroxine for 16 weeks). The proposition is untested for symptoms at historical doses and wholly untested for heart outcomes.

### (d) Thyroid hormone and thyroid-like drugs used as cholesterol or heart treatment
- Coronary Drug Project. Randomised, double-blind, funded by the US National Heart Institute, with total mortality over five years as its aim; 8,341 men aged 30 to 64 with a previous heart attack, enrolled March 1966 to October 1969; dextrothyroxine 6.0 mg/day (n = 1,110) vs lactose placebo (n = 2,789) [04.25][04.80]. The arm was stopped late in 1971 "because of an excess number of deaths" [04.25]. I could not obtain the 1972 report beyond its opening lines, so the death counts at stopping are not verified here [04.26]. Secondary accounts say the stop came after a mean 36 months, that the overall difference was not statistically significant, and that excess deaths clustered in men with angina, heart failure or fast heart rates [04.31][04.32]. The trial group later described testing subgroups "identified a posteriori" [04.28]. At 15 years mortality was similar to placebo [04.27]. One secondary summary gives "excess cancer mortality" as the reason, which contradicts the trial's custodian [04.33][04.25].
- Dose. In six euthyroid patients the dose of dextrothyroxine that suppressed the TSH response averaged 3.5 mg; the authors concluded the trial's 6 mg may have caused "mild, undetected hyperthyroidism" [04.29].
- Contamination. A 1984 assay of the marketed tablets (Choloxin) found 0.50% to 2.30% levothyroxine, enough to supply 33% to 53% of serum thyroxine at 8 mg/day [04.30]. At 6 mg/day that range equals 30 to 138 µg of levothyroxine daily (my arithmetic). The lots assayed were not the trial's own supply, so contamination of the trial drug is an inference. Either way the arm tested thyroid-hormone excess in men with damaged hearts. It did not test replacement in hypothyroid people. Choloxin (approved 14 April 1967) is listed as discontinued (checked 21 November 2026) [04.34].
- Barnes, Lancet 1959, read in full. About 700 private patients; 80 with cholesterol above 200 mg/100 ml were given desiccated thyroid 65 to 260 mg daily (usually 130 mg), guided by basal temperature and symptoms. Mean cholesterol fell from 242 to 180 mg/100 ml. There was no control group and four heart-attack cases. The paper states: "Sufficient time has not elapsed for significant observations on the incidence of arteriosclerosis" [04.35]. It is a cholesterol series, not an outcome study.
- Kountz. The 1950 paper is an autopsy series of 17 people [04.36]. The 1951 monograph, which I could not obtain, is described by Barnes as 280 cases, "some kept as untreated controls", with fewer vascular accidents and deaths over 5 years [04.35]; a blog describes 268 subjects in three groups [04.37]. How the controls were chosen is not stated in anything I could read. Wren 1971 is described by the same blog as 347 patients all given desiccated thyroid, with deaths compared against an expected number (11 vs 53 after "rough adjustment") [04.37]. These are leads, not findings. Such series can show that the doses lowered cholesterol and were tolerated by patients who stayed in one practice. They cannot show prevention, because the patients were self-selected, the comparison rates came from other populations, and outcomes were not ascertained blind.
- Modern thyroid-like drugs lower LDL but have no outcome data. Eprotirome: LDL fell 22% to 32% at 12 weeks on top of statins [04.39]; the phase 3 trial (236 randomised) was stopped after cartilage damage in dogs, with raised liver enzymes in patients [04.40]. Resmetirom: LDL −13.6% and −16.3% vs 0.1% on placebo at week 24 in 966 patients with fatty liver disease [04.41]; FDA accelerated approval on 14 March 2024 for liver disease only [04.42]; the label revised 07/2026 still makes continued approval contingent on verifying clinical benefit [04.43]. DITPA in heart failure (n = 86): LDL −30%, poorly tolerated, no symptomatic benefit [04.44]. Triiodothyronine after heart attack: two pilot trials of 37 patients each, imaging endpoints only, with atrial fibrillation in 19% vs 5% in one [04.45][04.46].

- Harm is concentrated at a suppressed TSH (below 0.1, or 0.03 and below). On thyroid hormone, in the band between about 0.1 and 0.4 the excess is small (13% for cardiovascular death in veterans) or not detectable (Tayside, THIN). This does not hold for untreated people: Framingham, a general-population cohort, found no significant atrial fibrillation excess at 0.1 to 0.4, but the pooled cohorts of untreated people with a normal free T4 found atrial fibrillation HR 1.63 at 0.10 to 0.44 [04.51][04.53], and thread 06 adds total mortality HR 1.23 in that band. Sources disagree on rhythm disturbance and fracture in treated patients: Tayside found dysrhythmia and fracture raised at suppressed TSH; THIN found no link with atrial fibrillation or fracture [04.47][04.48].
- Duration matters. Each 6 months of low TSH on levothyroxine raised mortality by a factor of 1.18 (1.15 to 1.21), against 1.05 per 6 months of raised TSH [04.50]. In people not taking thyroid medication, each 6 months with TSH below 0.3 raised hip fracture risk by a factor of 1.07 [04.55].
- Other signals: hip fracture RR 3.6 and vertebral fracture OR 4.5 at TSH 0.1 or below in women over 65, with no excess from thyroid hormone use when TSH was normal [04.52]; fractures rising with dose in Ontario adults aged 70 or over (OR 3.45 for more than 93 µg vs under 44 µg daily) and atrial fibrillation rising with dose in those aged 66 or over (OR 1.29 for 125 µg or more vs under 75 µg) [04.60][04.61]; cognitive disorder HR 1.34 (1.10 to 1.63) with exogenous thyrotoxicosis after 65 [04.57]; in thyroid cancer patients kept suppressed, cardiovascular death rose 3.08-fold per 10-fold fall in TSH [04.56].
- Over-replacement is frequent in practice: 41% of US thyroid hormone users aged 65 or over had a TSH below 0.45 (1989 to 1990) [04.62]; pooled recent estimate 20% [04.63].
- These are observational. A low TSH on treatment also marks weight loss, frailty and deliberate suppression for cancer, and no trial has randomised people to a suppressed TSH for outcomes. A 2026 review of 12 studies found adverse events mainly when TSH was suppressed [04.77].
- Triiodothyronine and desiccated thyroid: no outcome trials. Cohort data show no excess of atrial fibrillation, heart failure or stroke with liothyronine (17-year Scottish study; meta-analysis of cohorts totalling 630,254 people) [04.69][04.70]. Blinded crossover trials show no overall symptom advantage; desiccated thyroid raised heart rate slightly [04.71]. A systematic review found long-term safety data lacking [04.72]. The maker's trial of Armour Thyroid (n = 2,701) has a TSH endpoint [04.73].

### The trials that have never been run
1. Levothyroxine vs placebo for heart events and deaths in subclinical hypothyroidism, sized in the thousands and run for years, especially under 65 or with TSH 7 to 20. TRUST was planned as this and was cut. A 2026 European Heart Journal review says such trials "have not been conducted to date and may not be feasible" [04.76].
2. Symptom-titrated thyroid hormone vs placebo in symptomatic adults with a normal TSH, lasting a year or more and counting atrial fibrillation, fractures and heart events.
3. Thyroid-first vs statin-first in adults with raised LDL and a borderline TSH.
4. A lower vs higher TSH target within the reference range, for outcomes.
- Registry check on 21 November 2026: the only registered trials with heart events as the primary outcome were a 700-person Chinese trial (status unknown, last updated January 2023) and a 338-person trial after bypass surgery [04.74][04.75].

## What this means for the person's situation
- The evidence supports finding and treating a clearly underactive thyroid (overt hypothyroidism, or TSH of 10 or more) before judging cholesterol. No trial proves that treatment prevents heart attacks, but untreated and under-treated states carry the worst outcomes in every dataset, and treatment of overt disease is not disputed.
- For a mildly raised TSH (about 4.5 to 10) at 65 or over, trials show no symptom benefit and are too small to show anything about heart events. Many such results return to normal on re-testing. Under 65, or with TSH 7 to 10 and above, there is no trial evidence at all; observational studies lean towards fewer events, with known bias.
- For hypothyroid symptoms with a normal TSH, no blinded study has found thyroid hormone better than placebo, and no study has measured heart events or deaths. "Treat thyroid first by symptoms, then reconsider the statin" is unproven, not disproven. Anyone weighing it should know it rests on uncontrolled series.
- Where the TSH ends up is the main safety question. Most measurable harm sits at a TSH below 0.1: roughly 20% to 40% higher rates of death or cardiovascular events, two to three times the atrial fibrillation in older people, and more fractures. On thyroid hormone, a low but detectable TSH shows little or no excess; in untreated people the same band carries more atrial fibrillation (thread 06). Risks grow with age and with time spent suppressed.
- Thyroid hormone is not a cholesterol drug for people with normal thyroid function. The one large trial of that idea was stopped for excess deaths, and the newer thyroid-like drugs have no heart-outcome data.
- Assumptions. The report assumes a US reader; drug labels, approvals and the veterans data are US-specific, and UK or European practice differs in thresholds (see thread 05). It also assumes "historical" means diagnosis by symptoms and signs. If it instead meant historical doses (several grains of desiccated thyroid), the over-replacement findings apply more directly, because such doses often suppress TSH (see thread 02).

## Load-bearing claims
- C04.1: The only placebo-controlled trials of levothyroxine in subclinical hypothyroidism that counted events pooled 842 people aged 65 or over (mean TSH 6.4) for a median 17 months: 44 cardiovascular events (HR 0.74, 0.41 to 1.35) and 21 deaths (HR 1.28, 0.54 to 3.03). TRUST was cut from a planned 3,000 and its report says it cannot exclude protection or harm. [04.5][04.1][04.3]
- C04.2: I found no placebo-controlled trial that counted heart events or deaths for thyroid hormone in overt hypothyroidism, in adults under 65 with subclinical hypothyroidism, or in people with symptoms and a normal TSH; on 21 November 2026 the only registered trials with events as their primary outcome were open-label, with no placebo: NCT01848171 (700 planned, ages 40 to 75, overt or subclinical hypothyroidism; status unknown, no results posted) and NCT07380854 (338 planned, people having bypass surgery, events within 4 months). [04.7][04.76][04.74][04.75]
- C04.3: In symptom-defined patients, three blinded placebo-controlled studies (1960, n = 18; 1960, n = 20; 2001, n = 22 completing) found thyroid hormone no better than placebo. The two 1960 studies chose patients with a low basal metabolic rate whose protein-bound iodine and radioiodine uptake were normal (one allocated by rotation, one only partly blinded); the 2001 trial required thyroid tests in the reference range. The main comparisons ran 4 to 12 weeks, and none counted events. [04.19][04.20][04.21]
- C04.4: The Coronary Drug Project gave dextrothyroxine 6 mg/day to 1,110 men after heart attack (placebo 2,789) and stopped the arm in late 1971 for excess deaths; the dose was about 1.7 times the 3.5 mg that suppresses TSH, and tablets of the marketed brand later assayed at 0.50% to 2.30% levothyroxine. On these figures the arm tested thyroid excess, not replacement; that reading is mine, the 3.5 mg figure is a mean from 6 patients, and the trial's own tablet supply was not assayed. [04.25][04.29][04.30]
- C04.5: On treatment, cardiovascular death was HR 1.39 at TSH below 0.1, 1.13 at 0.1 to 0.5 and 2.67 at TSH above 20 (705,307 US veterans); in Tayside, TSH 0.03 or below carried HR 1.37 for cardiovascular disease and 2.02 for fracture, while 0.04 to 0.4 showed no significant excess. [04.49][04.47]
- C04.6: Observational studies associate levothyroxine for subclinical hypothyroidism with fewer events in younger adults (HR 0.61 at ages 40 to 70; pooled mortality RR 0.50 under 65 to 70) but not in adults over 65 to 70 in those two sources. Two analyses of one UK database of adults over 50 gave mortality HRs of 1.17 and 0.71, and both reported fewer cardiovascular events with levothyroxine (HR 0.91 and 0.82). [04.9][04.12][04.14][04.15]

## Confidence Notes
- Firm: the randomised evidence on heart events and deaths is too small to show an effect either way; the investigators say so themselves [04.1]. Firm: a suppressed TSH is associated with more cardiovascular death, atrial fibrillation and fracture, with a dose-response and replication across independent groups in Scotland, England, Denmark and the US.
- Moderate: on thyroid hormone, the band between 0.1 and 0.4 is close to neutral. Two treated datasets agree, but all are observational and one large US dataset shows a 13% excess. In untreated people the same band is not neutral for atrial fibrillation [04.53].
- Weak: any benefit of treating subclinical hypothyroidism on heart events in younger adults. It rests on observational data, and the ACEL-UK pair shows the answer can reverse with method [04.14][04.15].
- Thin and old: evidence in symptom-defined patients. Three small blinded studies, two from 1960, none testing the historical titration method.
- Unverified: the Coronary Drug Project's death counts at stopping. The 1972 paper is paywalled and I read only its opening lines. Descriptions of Kountz 1951 and Wren 1971 come from Barnes's one-paragraph summary and a blog.
- "Read: full" means the full text was obtained and the sections bearing on the claims were read. For [04.4], [04.6], [04.7], [04.14], [04.19], [04.31], [04.32] and [04.53] the text came through a fetch tool that summarises; figures were cross-checked against abstracts where possible. Scanned sources [04.20], [04.21], [04.35] and [04.36] were read as OCR text, with some table values illegible.
- Internal inconsistencies noted in sources: the pooled-trial abstract prints the cardiovascular confidence interval as 0.41 to 1.25, the results text as 0.41 to 1.35 (the latter is used) [04.5]; two abstracts print confidence intervals that look like typographical errors [04.9][04.47].
- Distortions. Funding: levothyroxine is off patent; TRUST was publicly funded and shrank for want of placebo tablets. Circularity: the trials in section (c) from 2001 onward define "normal" by TSH; the 1960 studies used metabolic rate and protein-bound iodine and reached the same result. Sellers on both sides: eprotirome, resmetirom and Armour trials are maker-funded; the historical series were written by practitioners committed to the treatment. One 2026 systematic review on this exact question has been retracted and is not used [04.78].
- No page I read contained instructions addressed to an automated reader. Several publisher sites returned bot-verification pages; I did not attempt to pass them.

## Gaps
- Not obtained: the Coronary Drug Project 1972 JAMA report and the 1970 report (paywalled); Kountz's 1951 monograph; Wren 1968 and 1971 and Barnes 1973 (J Am Geriatr Soc; publisher blocked, not in the Internet Archive scans, which stop at 1961); Keating 1961 on angina in treated myxoedema; the full NEJM text of TRUST and Flynn 2010. A library request for these would settle the unverified figures.
- Looked for and not found: any randomised trial of thyroid hormone vs placebo or no treatment with heart events or deaths in overt hypothyroidism, in adults under 65 with subclinical hypothyroidism, or in symptomatic people with normal TSH. Searched PubMed and Europe PMC (terms combining levothyroxine, liothyronine, desiccated thyroid, euthyroid, symptoms, randomised, placebo, cardiovascular, mortality) and ClinicalTrials.gov (subclinical hypothyroidism with levothyroxine, active statuses; interventional thyroid hormone trials of 500 or more). Likely reasons: withholding treatment in overt disease is unethical; the drug is off patent; event rates in younger adults are low, so a trial would need many thousands for many years.
- Not studied at all: hard outcomes of symptom-titrated treatment; hard outcomes of desiccated thyroid or combination therapy; a randomised comparison of TSH targets for outcomes; thyroid-first vs statin-first.
- Belongs to other threads. Thread 01: supraphysiological levothyroxine and lipids in healthy volunteers (Sjouke 2017, Sci Rep, not read). Thread 02: the 1956 to 1960 "metabolic insufficiency" episode, in which uncontrolled series of up to 51 patients reported triiodothyronine cures that two blinded studies then failed to confirm [04.20][04.21]. Thread 03: Mendelian randomisation work on TSH and heart disease summarised in [04.76]. Thread 05: statin use rose from 19.1% to 24.6% after levothyroxine was started despite a normalised TSH [04.79].
- What would strengthen this thread: individual-participant event data from TRUST by age and baseline TSH; the full ACEL-UK target-trial paper; an independent replication of the US veterans' TSH-band analysis in a population that is not 88% male.

# Thyroid function and cholesterol — Research brief

Confirmed 2026-11-21. This is the first of two linked runs. The second, `cholesterol-lowering-mortality`, asks whether lowering cholesterol reduces death from any cause.

## Question
1. How does thyroid function affect cholesterol, and how large is the effect?
2. Should doctors bring thyroid function to a healthy level before prescribing cholesterol-lowering drugs, where "healthy" is judged by the standard used before the modern TSH test (diagnosis by symptoms and signs) and not only by today's TSH reference ranges?
3. Is TSH itself harmful, for example by driving inflammation, so that keeping it lower than current practice does would be better?

## What this is for
A public evidence review, released as a worked example with the knowledge-superpowers research skill. A reader should be able to use it when weighing a cholesterol-lowering drug or a thyroid work-up, and to check every claim against its source.

## Your situation
- No individual's situation is used. The report is written for a general adult reader and contains no personal information about anyone.
- Country: US guidelines first, with UK and European guidelines for comparison (assumption).
- Starting position supplied with the request. It is to be tested, not assumed:
  - "Historical" means diagnosis by symptoms and signs before the modern TSH test.
  - There is a body of research showing that TSH is pro-inflammatory, so keeping TSH lower than modern practice does has support from several angles.

## Deliverable
Both: a sourced reference, and a decision section written for any adult who has been offered a cholesterol-lowering drug or wonders whether thyroid has been properly ruled out. For public release. Markdown only.

## Scope
- **In:** adults; overt and subclinical hypothyroidism, low-normal thyroid function, and people with hypothyroid symptoms whose TSH is normal; LDL, total cholesterol, apoB, Lp(a) and triglycerides; diagnosis and dosing practice from about 1900 to now; effects of TSH outside the thyroid; outcomes of thyroid hormone treatment (lipids, heart events, deaths) and the harms of over-replacement; what guidelines require before a cholesterol-lowering drug and what is done in practice.
- **Out:** whether lowering cholesterol reduces death from any cause (the companion run); individual medical advice (the report gives evidence and questions for a doctor); children and pregnancy; thyroid cancer and hyperthyroidism, except where they show what a low TSH or excess thyroid hormone does.
- **Time window:** evidence from any year; guidelines, drug labels and approvals as of November 2026.

## Bias note
Produced by the blind-variables pass on 2026-11-21.

### Information landscape
- **Strong coverage:** overt hypothyroidism raising LDL and treatment reversing it; guideline text; harms of a suppressed TSH; cohort studies of subclinical hypothyroidism.
- **Weak coverage:** trials of thyroid treatment that count heart events or deaths; anything that enrols patients by symptoms; pre-1975 practice, which is poorly indexed and has no abstracts; how often thyroid is checked before a statin.
- **Distorted coverage:** the TSH reference range, a statistical convention often presented as physiology; studies that judge symptoms against TSH, the test that replaced them; second-hand retellings of Barnes and Peat; cell and animal work on TSH and inflammation that comes from a few groups.

### Key distortions
1. Thyroid hormone is off-patent, so no sponsor pays for outcome trials, while lipid drugs have dozens. The gap in evidence reflects money and says nothing about truth (research economics).
2. Since the 1970s hypothyroidism has been defined by TSH, so later studies cannot see disease with a normal TSH, and trials such as TRUST get applied beyond the older, mildly affected people they enrolled (institutional amplification).
3. The other side has sellers too: desiccated thyroid, clinics, supplements and books (research economics).
4. Patients, mostly women, with symptoms and normal results have been dismissed, and doctors who treated by symptoms have been disciplined. Advocacy sources over-claim in reply (social filtering).
5. A language model defaults to current guidelines, has thin data on the pre-TSH era and may invent its details, and tends to confirm the hypothesis it is handed (AI filters).

### Compensation strategy
- Read pre-1975 papers in the original and record criteria and doses exactly.
- On TSH harm, favour human studies that separate TSH from thyroid hormone: injected TSH, long-term TSH suppression, pituitary hypothyroidism.
- Seek out findings against the starting position, such as cohorts where a higher TSH goes with longer life.
- Flag circular validation, funders, and whether independent groups have replicated a finding.
- Name the trial that would settle each open question.

### Confidence calibration
- Expected to be firm: the link between overt hypothyroidism and LDL; the harms of over-replacement.
- Uncertain until shown otherwise: benefit on heart events or deaths from treating mild or symptom-defined hypothyroidism; TSH as a direct cause of harm in people.

## Threads
1. `01-thyroid-cholesterol-link` — How thyroid status changes blood lipids, by what mechanism, how large the effect is from overt to subclinical to low-normal function, and how much treatment reverses it. Sets how big a lever thyroid is.
2. `02-symptom-based-diagnosis-before-tsh` — How hypothyroidism was diagnosed and dosed before the TSH test, how many people that standard treated compared with today's, what changed when TSH testing arrived and why, how well symptoms identify the disease, and what Barnes and similar writers claim against the original papers. Pins down "historically healthy" so it can be tested.
3. `03-tsh-direct-effects` — Whether TSH itself, apart from thyroid hormone levels, drives inflammation, cholesterol production or artery disease, and what TSH level goes with the best outcomes. Tests the claim that a lower TSH is better, for and against.
4. `04-thyroid-treatment-outcomes` — What giving thyroid hormone does to cholesterol, heart events and deaths: in overt and subclinical hypothyroidism, in people with symptoms and a normal TSH, and in the trials that used thyroid hormone or thyroid-mimicking drugs as cholesterol treatment; and the harms of over-replacement. Carries the strongest case against thyroid-first treatment by symptoms.
5. `05-thyroid-check-before-lipid-drugs` — What guidelines and drug labels say about testing and treating thyroid before a cholesterol-lowering drug, how often it is done, how common missed hypothyroidism is among people with raised cholesterol or starting statins, what goes wrong when it is missed, and what test, treat and retest costs in time and money. The direct answer to "should doctors do this first", and the practical side.

Every thread also reports what has not been studied and why.

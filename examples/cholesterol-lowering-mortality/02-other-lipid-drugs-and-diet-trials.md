# Thread 02: Death from any cause in trials of non-statin cholesterol lowering (drugs, surgery, diet)

## Question
What do randomised trials of every way of lowering cholesterol other than statins show for death from any cause? Cover ezetimibe, PCSK9 antibodies, inclisiran, bempedoic acid, CETP inhibitors, fibrates, niacin, bile-acid resins, the older drugs (clofibrate, oestrogen, dextrothyroxine), ileal bypass surgery, and cholesterol-lowering diet trials. Does the pattern across methods suggest that lowering LDL cholesterol itself extends life, or that any effect depends on the method?

## Key Findings

### Short answer
- No non-statin method has shown fewer deaths from any cause as the planned main result of a trial. The modern drug trials were sized for cardiovascular events, with death from any cause as a secondary, tertiary or safety outcome. Two older trials that made total mortality a main outcome (Coronary Drug Project, POSCH) showed no significant reduction while they ran [02.59][02.65].
- One large trial now shows fewer deaths from any cause with a non-statin drug: VESALIUS-CV (evolocumab; published 2025 to 2026), though under the trial's own testing order the result is nominal, not formally significant. One more shows a nominal reduction (ODYSSEY OUTCOMES, alirocumab). The rest are neutral, and several older or failed agents had more deaths in the treatment arm.
- The effect on death from any cause depends on the method, the population and the length of treatment. Lowering LDL by a given amount did not produce the same result for deaths across methods: torcetrapib lowered LDL by 24.9% and raised deaths; bempedoic acid and ezetimibe lowered LDL and left deaths unchanged; evolocumab left deaths unchanged at 2.2 years in one population and lowered them at 4.6 years in another.

Figures below are deaths from any cause, treatment arm first, unless a sentence says "cardiovascular death". "per 1,000" figures marked (calc.) are my arithmetic from the published counts.

### Ezetimibe

| SHARP (2011) | Ezetimibe + simvastatin vs placebo in chronic kidney disease; 9,270; median 4.9 years | 1,142 of 4,650 vs 1,115 of 4,620 | RR 1.02 (0.94 to 1.11); non-vascular deaths 668 vs 612 [02.2] | Merck/Schering-Plough plus public funders; run by Oxford; completed [02.2] |

IMPROVE-IT is the best test of ezetimibe: six years, 2,446 deaths, an LDL difference of about 16 mg/dL, and no difference in deaths from any cause or in cardiovascular deaths (537 vs 538) [02.1]. A Cochrane review pooled eight trials (21,222 people) at RR 0.98 (0.91 to 1.05) for death from any cause [02.6]. A 2025 meta-analysis reporting fewer deaths with the combination (OR 0.81) mixed 11 randomised trials with 3 cohort studies [02.84].

### PCSK9 antibodies

| Trial | Design | Deaths, treatment vs control | Result | Funder; stopped early? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FOURIER (2017) | Evolocumab vs placebo on statin, established cardiovascular disease; 27,564; median 2.2 years | 444 of 13,784 vs 426 of 13,780 | HR 1.04 (0.91 to 1.19); cardiovascular death 251 vs 240, HR 1.05 (0.88 to 1.25) [02.8][02.9] | Amgen. Event-driven: the paper says follow-up was planned at about 4 years and ended sooner because events accrued faster [02.7] |
| ODYSSEY OUTCOMES (2018) | Alirocumab vs placebo after acute coronary syndrome; 18,924; median 2.8 years | 334 of 9,462 vs 392 of 9,462 | HR 0.85 (0.73 to 0.98), nominal P=0.03 [02.11][02.12] | Sanofi and Regeneron; completed [02.11] |
| SPIRE-1 and SPIRE-2 (2017) | Bococizumab vs placebo; 27,438; median 10 months | SPIRE-1: 72 vs 65; SPIRE-2: 54 vs 61 (safety populations) | HR 1.12 (0.79 to 1.60) and 0.91 (0.63 to 1.32) [02.14] | Pfizer; stopped by sponsor (antidrug antibodies) [02.13] |
| VESALIUS-CV (2025 to 2026) | Evolocumab vs placebo; atherosclerosis or high-risk diabetes, no prior heart attack or stroke, LDL at least 90 mg/dL (or non-HDL cholesterol at least 120 mg/dL, or apoB at least 80 mg/dL); 12,257; median 4.6 years | 434 of 6,129 vs 539 of 6,128 | HR 0.80 (0.70 to 0.91); 5-year rates 7.9% vs 9.7% [02.16][02.17] | Amgen; completed July 2025 [02.15][02.17] |

- FOURIER could not have detected a modest effect either way: with 870 deaths its interval runs from 9% fewer to 19% more deaths [02.8]. A 2022 re-analysis of the regulatory study report (Erviti and colleagues) found the same totals (444 vs 426) but re-classified causes: cardiovascular deaths 150 vs 125 (RR 1.20, 0.95 to 1.51) and cardiac deaths 113 vs 88 (RR 1.28, 0.97 to 1.69), with 308 of 870 deaths judged undetermined. It describes the trial as "terminated early", which the trial paper does not [02.9][02.7]. In the open-label extension (6,635 of the original patients, median 5.0 further years), cardiovascular death was lower in those originally given evolocumab (HR 0.77, 0.60 to 0.99); the abstract gives no figure for death from any cause [02.10].
- ODYSSEY's result is nominal because death from any cause sat last in a testing hierarchy after coronary death and cardiovascular death, neither of which was significant (cardiovascular death 240 vs 271, HR 0.88, 0.74 to 1.05). Among 8,242 patients eligible for 3 or more years of follow-up the HR was 0.78 (0.65 to 0.94) [02.12]. Absolute difference: about 6 fewer deaths per 1,000 over 2.8 years (calc.).
- VESALIUS-CV is the strongest non-statin mortality result to date: 105 fewer deaths, about 18 fewer per 1,000 over five years on the published rates. Cardiovascular death was 156 vs 195 (HR 0.79, 0.64 to 0.98), non-cardiovascular death 229 vs 268 (HR 0.85, 0.71 to 1.01) and undetermined 49 vs 76 [02.16]. No difference appeared in the first 1.5 years (HR 0.99); afterwards the HR was 0.73 [02.19]. Caveats: the authors state the trial was not designed or powered for mortality; coronary death, which ranked earlier, was not significantly reduced (105 vs 117, HR 0.89, 0.68 to 1.16) [02.17]. The statistical analysis plan posted on the registry record tests secondary endpoints in a fixed order, with death from any cause ninth of ten after coronary death and cardiovascular death, so formal testing stopped at coronary death and the mortality P value is nominal [02.17]; the US label for evolocumab says the all-cause result "did not meet statistical significance according to the prespecified hierarchical testing procedure" and analyses 12,206 patients (432 of 6,106 vs 538 of 6,100) [02.86]; the sponsor ran the trial; and this population had LDL of at least 90 mg/dL, or a raised non-HDL cholesterol or apoB, despite treatment. The abstract states the result was consistent across age, sex, baseline LDL and background lipid therapy, without giving the subgroup numbers [02.16]. In the 3,655 patients with diabetes and no known significant atherosclerosis, deaths were 136 vs 172 (HR 0.76, 0.61 to 0.95) [02.18].
- Pooled estimates moved with VESALIUS-CV. Before it: Cochrane 2020 gave OR 1.04 (0.91 to 1.19) for evolocumab and 0.83 (0.72 to 0.96) for alirocumab [02.20]; a 2022 BMJ network meta-analysis gave RR 0.95 (0.87 to 1.03) for PCSK9 inhibitors and 0.99 (0.92 to 1.06) for ezetimibe added to statins [02.21]. After it: a meta-analysis published 2 November 2026 (32 trials, 77,178 participants) gives a rate ratio of 0.88 (0.82 to 0.95) for death from any cause, and its authors say the result rests mainly on antibody trials in people with known atherosclerosis and cannot be extended to inclisiran [02.22].

### Inclisiran, bempedoic acid and oral PCSK9 inhibitors
- Inclisiran has no outcome-trial result as of 21 November 2026. ORION-4 (16,124 people; University of Oxford with Novartis) is listed as active, not recruiting, primary completion estimated October 2026, no results posted [02.24]. VICTORION-2P (17,004) has primary completion estimated May 2027 and VICTORION-1P (14,012) April 2029 [02.25]. Novartis says it expects both secondary-prevention trials to report in 2027 [02.26]. The pooled phase 3 trials (3,655 people, 18 months) recorded 27 deaths in each arm [02.23].
- Bempedoic acid, CLEAR Outcomes (2023): 13,970 statin-intolerant people, median 40.6 months; deaths 434 of 6,992 vs 420 of 6,978, HR 1.03 (0.90 to 1.18); cardiovascular death 269 vs 257, HR 1.04 (0.88 to 1.24); funded by Esperion [02.27][02.28]. In the 4,206 primary-prevention patients deaths were 75 vs 109 (HR 0.73, 0.54 to 0.98) [02.29]. By subtraction the patients with established disease had 359 vs 311 deaths (calc.), so the subgroup result is offset in the rest of the trial and should be read as a subgroup finding.
- The oral PCSK9 inhibitor enlicitide has lipid results only; its outcomes trial (CORALreef Outcomes) has a primary completion date of November 2029 [02.85].

| dal-OUTCOMES (2012) | Dalcetrapib vs placebo; 15,871; median 31 months | 226 of 7,938 vs 229 of 7,933 | No difference [02.31][02.32] | Roche; stopped for futility [02.31] |
| ACCELERATE (2017) | Evacetrapib vs placebo; 12,092; median 26 months | 231 of 6,038 vs 276 of 6,054 | HR 0.84 (0.70 to 1.00), P=0.04 unadjusted; no effect on cardiovascular events [02.33] | Eli Lilly; stopped for futility [02.33] |
| REVEAL (2017) | Anacetrapib vs placebo on atorvastatin; 30,449; median 4.1 years | 1,122 of 15,225 vs 1,155 of 15,224 | No significant difference [02.34][02.35] | Merck and UK public funders; run by Oxford; completed [02.34] |

Torcetrapib lowered LDL by 24.9% and raised blood pressure by 5.4 mm Hg with changes in aldosterone and electrolytes; the excess deaths are attributed to that off-target effect, though the authors could not exclude a class effect [02.30][02.34]. Dalcetrapib barely changed LDL [02.31]. REVEAL's extended follow-up (median 6.3 years) found fewer coronary deaths (516 vs 582) and cardiovascular deaths (722 vs 796, P=0.05) with no difference in non-vascular deaths (860 vs 870) [02.36]. Obicetrapib's outcome trial PREVAIL (9,541 people) is listed with an estimated primary completion of November 2026 (record last updated June 2024) and no results; the 1-year BROADWAY trial recorded 19 deaths among 1,685 on obicetrapib and 12 among 843 on placebo, in 2:1 randomisation [02.37][02.38].

### Fibrates and niacin
- Helsinki Heart Study (gemfibrozil, 4,081 men without coronary disease, 5 years): no difference in total death rate in the trial; at 8.5 years deaths were 101 vs 83 (P=0.19), with cancer deaths 30 vs 18 [02.40][02.41].
- VA-HIT (gemfibrozil, 2,531 men with coronary disease, median 5.1 years): 198 of 1,264 vs 220 of 1,267 (15.7% vs 17.4%, P=0.23); the paper states it had 20% power to detect a 10% reduction in total mortality. Funded by the US Department of Veterans Affairs with a grant from Parke-Davis [02.42].
- BIP (bezafibrate, 3,090 with coronary disease, 6.2 years): total mortality did not differ between groups; the abstract gives no counts [02.43].
- FIELD (fenofibrate, 9,795 with type 2 diabetes, 5 years): 7.3% vs 6.6% (P=0.18), about 7 more deaths per 1,000 (calc.); coronary death HR 1.19 (0.90 to 1.57) [02.44].
- ACCORD-Lipid (fenofibrate + simvastatin, 5,518 with diabetes, 5 years; US government funded): 203 of 2,765 vs 221 of 2,753, HR 0.91 (0.75 to 1.10) [02.45].
- PROMINENT (pemafibrate, 10,497 with diabetes, median 3.4 years; Kowa; stopped for futility): 308 vs 310 deaths in the safety populations [02.46][02.47].
- Pooled: Cochrane reviews give RR 1.01 (0.81 to 1.26) in primary prevention and 0.98 (0.91 to 1.06) in secondary prevention [02.51][02.52]. A 2005 review found fibrates raised non-cardiovascular death (RR 1.13, 1.01 to 1.27) [02.81].
- Niacin: AIM-HIGH (3,414 people, stopped early for lack of efficacy; NHLBI and Abbott) recorded cardiovascular deaths of 45 vs 38; the count for death from any cause sits in a table I could not read [02.53]. HPS2-THRIVE (niacin-laropiprant, 25,673 people, median 3.9 years; Merck and UK public funders, run by Oxford): 798 of 12,838 vs 732 of 12,835, RR 1.09 (0.99 to 1.21), P=0.08, about 5 more deaths per 1,000 (calc.), with excess serious infection and bleeding [02.54]. Cochrane: RR 1.05 (0.97 to 1.12) across 12 trials [02.55]. An unfunded BMJ meta-analysis of niacin, fibrates and CETP inhibitors found no mortality effect for any class (OR 1.03, 0.98 and 1.16) [02.56].

### Older drugs, resins and surgery
- Coronary Drug Project (8,341 men after heart attack, 1966 to 1975, US government): high-dose oestrogen was stopped in 1970 for non-fatal adverse effects, dextrothyroxine in late 1971 because it had more deaths than placebo, and low-dose oestrogen in 1973 for no benefit [02.59]. Five-year mortality was 20.0% with clofibrate (1,103 men) vs 20.9% with placebo (2,789 men), P=0.55 [02.57]. Niacin did not reduce deaths during the trial, but at 15 years mortality was 52.0% vs 58.2% (P=0.0004), about nine years after treatment ended [02.58].
- WHO clofibrate trial (primary prevention, men with high cholesterol, 5.3 years of treatment): fewer heart attacks but significantly higher total mortality; the excess in the clofibrate group was 47% during treatment and 5% afterwards, and 11% (70 deaths) over 13.2 years [02.49][02.50]. At 9.6 years there were 25% more deaths (P<0.01), spread across causes other than accidents [02.48]. The in-trial counts usually quoted (162 vs 127) are a lead I could not read in the 1978 report.
- Smaller clofibrate trials differed: Stockholm (clofibrate plus niacin, open-label) had 61 of 279 vs 82 of 276 deaths (P<0.05) [02.60]; the Newcastle trial reported a lower death rate, and the Scottish trial fewer deaths only among patients with angina [02.61][02.62].
- Cholestyramine, LRC-CPPT (3,806 men, 7.4 years, US government): death from any cause 7% lower, not significant, because of more violent and accidental deaths in the treatment group [02.63]; at 13.4 years deaths were 143 vs 156 (HR 0.89, not significant) [02.64].
- Partial ileal bypass, POSCH (838 heart-attack survivors, LDL 37.7% lower): 49 vs 62 deaths at 9.7 years (P=0.164); significant by 14.7 years (P=0.049); 25-year survival 57% vs 51%, an estimated gain of 1.0 year of life [02.65][02.66][02.67].

The recovered Minnesota data confirmed no mortality benefit and, among the 2,355 people on the diets for a year or more, linked each 30 mg/dL fall in cholesterol to a 22% higher risk of death (HR 1.22, 1.14 to 1.32). That is an observational comparison within the trial, concentrated in people aged 65 and over [02.69]; in my reading it is open to reverse causation, because illness lowers cholesterol. The Finnish Mental Hospital Study reported lower coronary and total mortality in men but allocated two hospitals in a crossover, not individuals [02.72][02.77]. The Women's Health Initiative diet (48,835 women, 8.1 years) lowered LDL by only 3.55 mg/dL and did not change coronary disease or stroke [02.73]. The Cochrane review of saturated-fat reduction (2020) found RR 0.96 (0.90 to 1.03) for death from any cause (11 trials, 55,858 people) and 0.95 (0.80 to 1.12) for cardiovascular death, with a 17% reduction in combined cardiovascular events [02.76]. Analyses limited to the early trials give RR 1.07 (0.90 to 1.27) for five trials replacing saturated fat with linoleic-acid oils [02.69] and 0.996 (0.865 to 1.147) for six trials published by 1978 [02.71].

### Pooled analyses across methods
- For cardiovascular events (not deaths), a 2016 meta-regression found the same relative reduction per 1 mmol/L of LDL lowering for statins (RR 0.77) and for diet, resins, ileal bypass and ezetimibe together (RR 0.75); CETP inhibitors did worse than their LDL lowering predicted [02.78].
- For deaths, a 2018 meta-analysis (34 trials, 270,288 people) found death from any cause lower with more intensive LDL lowering (RR 0.92, 0.88 to 0.96), with the reduction confined to trials whose baseline LDL was 100 mg/dL or more [02.79]. A 2023 meta-analysis (60 trials of statins, ezetimibe and PCSK9 antibodies) found the same overall RR of 0.92 but no further reduction in deaths with larger percentage falls in LDL (RR 1.00, 0.94 to 1.06) [02.80].
- The older reviews found total mortality unchanged in primary-prevention trials [02.82], benefit only at very high baseline coronary risk (OR 0.74) with harm at low risk (OR 1.22), and raised non-coronary mortality with the drugs of that era (OR 1.21) [02.83]. A 2005 review gave RR for death from any cause of 0.87 for statins, 1.00 fibrates, 0.84 (0.66 to 1.08) resins, 0.96 niacin and 0.97 diet [02.81].

### What the pattern suggests
The trials do not support "lowering LDL by any means extends life". They are consistent with a narrower statement: fewer deaths from any cause appear where vascular death is a large share of all deaths, LDL starts high, the fall is large and sustained for several years, and the agent has no offsetting toxicity (VESALIUS-CV, ODYSSEY's longer-treated patients, POSCH at 15 to 25 years). Short trials, low starting LDL, small LDL differences and populations dying mostly of other causes gave neutral results (FOURIER, IMPROVE-IT, SHARP, CLEAR Outcomes, REVEAL in-trial). Excess deaths came from agents with other actions (torcetrapib, clofibrate, dextrothyroxine, oestrogen, niacin-laropiprant) and from one diet trial (Sydney). Size matters: on the standard formula about 2,800 deaths are needed for 80% power to detect a 10% relative reduction, 1,200 for 15% and 630 for 20% (calc.); FOURIER (870 deaths), CLEAR Outcomes (854) and ODYSSEY (726) had that power only for reductions of roughly 17% to 19% or more (calc.).

## What this means for the person's situation
For an adult offered a non-statin cholesterol drug, the trial record on staying alive is as follows.
- Ezetimibe: fewer heart attacks and strokes when added to a statin after a coronary event, with no change in deaths over six years.
- PCSK9 antibodies: no change in deaths at about two years in people with established disease on statins (FOURIER); a nominal reduction after acute coronary syndrome (about 6 per 1,000 over 2.8 years); and about 18 fewer deaths per 1,000 over five years in high-risk people whose LDL (or non-HDL cholesterol or apoB) stayed raised on treatment (VESALIUS-CV). That last figure applies to people like those enrolled (median age 66, atherosclerosis or high-risk diabetes), not to low-risk adults.
- Bempedoic acid: fewer cardiovascular events in statin-intolerant people, no change in deaths overall.
- Inclisiran, obicetrapib and oral PCSK9 inhibitors: no evidence yet on deaths.
- Fibrates and niacin: no reduction in deaths; niacin-laropiprant and some older fibrates had more.
- Diet: replacing saturated fat lowers cholesterol and probably cardiovascular events, with little or no effect on deaths in trials.

The starting position, that cholesterol lowering "may no longer be advisable" when judged by death from any cause, is not supported for evolocumab in the VESALIUS-CV population and is supported, in the sense of no demonstrated survival gain, for ezetimibe, bempedoic acid, fibrates, niacin and diet. An absent mortality benefit in a two-to-four-year trial is not evidence of none. None of these findings depends on the assumption about country; which drugs are licensed, reimbursed or recommended does (threads on guidelines).

## Confidence Notes
- Read levels: "full" means I retrieved the full text (PubMed Central, a repository copy, or an Internet Archive snapshot) and read the passages and tables on deaths, stopping and funding, not every paragraph. NEJM pages returned a bot check, which I did not work around; archived NEJM pages show tables as images, so counts for FOURIER, SEAS, dal-OUTCOMES, REVEAL and CLEAR Outcomes come from ClinicalTrials.gov results records or other papers, as cited. Registry safety-population counts can differ by a few deaths from intention-to-treat counts (CLEAR Outcomes lists 437 of 7,001 as-treated; SPIRE and PROMINENT counts are safety populations).
- Firm: counts and hazard ratios for IMPROVE-IT, SHARP, FOURIER, ODYSSEY, VESALIUS-CV, CLEAR Outcomes, ILLUMINATE, ACCELERATE, REVEAL, HPS2-THRIVE, ACCORD-Lipid, VA-HIT, POSCH, Minnesota and Sydney, each checked against the paper, a registry record or both.
- Thin: older trials known to me only through abstracts (Helsinki, BIP, FIELD, LRC-CPPT, WHO clofibrate, Coronary Drug Project, Finnish, Women's Health Initiative). Early diet-trial counts other than Minnesota and Sydney come from one secondary source [02.71] whose lead author sells diet books. I did not verify funders for Helsinki, BIP, FIELD, EWTOPIA 75, WHO, Los Angeles Veterans or the Women's Health Initiative.
- VESALIUS-CV: I read abstracts, the registry record and trade-press coverage, not the full papers. At verification on 21 November 2026 the checker read the statistical analysis plan on the registry record and the current US label, which together establish that the all-cause mortality result is nominal under the prespecified testing order [02.17][02.86]. The trial was sponsor-run and has no independent re-analysis yet.
- Contradictions: FOURIER "terminated early" (Erviti) versus event-driven completion (trial paper). A search snippet of an American College of Cardiology summary gives ACCELERATE mortality as 3.8% vs 4.1% (P=0.06); the published table gives 231 (3.8%) vs 276 (4.6%), P=0.04. Pooled PCSK9 estimates differ before and after VESALIUS-CV.
- Bias direction: nearly all modern trials were sponsor-funded and sized for composite events. Publicly funded or academically run: ACCORD-Lipid, LRC-CPPT, Coronary Drug Project, POSCH, Minnesota; co-funded with industry: AIM-HIGH, VA-HIT, SHARP, HPS2-THRIVE, REVEAL. The sceptic-side sources [02.9][02.69][02.70][02.71][02.80] report the same death totals as the trial papers; they differ on cause-of-death classification and interpretation.
- (calc.) figures and power estimates are my arithmetic. No page I read contained instructions addressed to an automated reader.

## Gaps
- Not found: an outcome-trial result for inclisiran or obicetrapib as of 21 November 2026 (ClinicalTrials.gov records, PubMed, web searches for ORION-4, VICTORION-2P and PREVAIL); the full VESALIUS-CV papers; arm-level deaths from any cause for AIM-HIGH, Helsinki, BIP, LRC-CPPT and WHO clofibrate (in-trial), the 1975 Coronary Drug Project report, the Upjohn colestipol trial and the Women's Health Initiative (tables behind bot checks, or 1970s papers without abstracts); Cochrane full texts.
- Not studied: no modern non-statin drug trial has death from any cause as its primary outcome; none is in low-risk primary prevention; EWTOPIA 75 is the only trial confined to people aged 75 and over, and it was open-label; women were 24% of participants across the 49 statin and non-statin trials in [02.78] and 43% in VESALIUS-CV, and I found no non-statin trial that prints deaths from any cause separately for women, apart from the all-female diet trial.

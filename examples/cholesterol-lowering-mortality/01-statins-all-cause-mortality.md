# Statin trials and death from any cause

## Question
What do randomised statin trials and their pooled analyses show for death from any cause? Primary and secondary prevention are reported separately, and by age (especially over 75), sex and baseline risk, in absolute terms: deaths in each arm, deaths avoided per 1,000 people treated over the trial, and any estimate of time gained. Beside this are the harms that bear on net mortality (new diabetes, bleeding stroke, muscle damage, any cancer signal).

## Key Findings

"Deaths avoided per 1,000" below is my own subtraction from the counts shown, over the trial's follow-up. It is a crude difference in proportions, not the trial's own statistic. "Death" always means death from any cause unless a cause is named.

### 1. People who already have cardiovascular disease: fewer deaths, shown in three large trials

| Trial, population | Follow-up | Died, statin | Died, control | Fewer deaths per 1,000 | Trial's own result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4S (1994): coronary disease, cholesterol 5.5–8.0 mmol/L | median 5.4 y | 182/2,221 (8.2%) | 256/2,223 (11.5%) | 33 | RR 0.70 (0.58–0.85) [01.1][01.2] |
| CARE (1996): after heart attack, average cholesterol | 5 y | 180/2,081 (8.7%) | 196/2,078 (9.4%) | 8 | 9% lower, CI −12 to 26, P=0.37 [01.3][01.5] |
| LIPID (1998): heart attack or unstable angina | mean 6.1 y | 498/4,512 (11.0%) | 633/4,502 (14.1%) | 30 | 22% lower (13–31) [01.4][01.5] |
| HPS (2002): coronary or other arterial disease, or diabetes | 5 y scheduled | 1,328/10,269 (12.9%) | 1,507/10,267 (14.7%) | 17–18 | P=0.0003 [01.6] |

In 4S and HPS the fall came from coronary or vascular deaths, and deaths from other causes did not differ (4S: 46 v 49 non-cardiovascular deaths; HPS: 547 v 570 non-vascular deaths, P=0.4) [01.1][01.6]. In LIPID, cardiovascular deaths were 331 v 433 [01.5]. CARE was sized for coronary events, not deaths (80% power for a 20% fall in its primary endpoint) [01.3]. Statins did not lower deaths in the heart-failure trials: GISSI-HF 28.8% v 28.1% died (RR 1.00, 0.90–1.12) and CORONA 29.0% v 30.4% (RR 0.95, 0.86–1.05) [01.46].

### 2. People without known cardiovascular disease, and mixed populations: small or no difference in each trial

| Trial, population | Follow-up | Died, statin | Died, control | Fewer deaths per 1,000 | Trial's own result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| WOSCOPS (1995): men 45–64, high cholesterol | 4.9 y | 106/3,302 (3.2%) | 135/3,293 (4.1%) | 9 | 22% lower (0–40), P=0.051 [01.8][01.5] |
| AFCAPS/TexCAPS (1998): average cholesterol, low HDL | 5.2 y | 80/3,304 (2.4%) | 77/3,301 (2.3%) | −1 | RR 1.04 (0.76–1.42) [01.10][01.11][01.42] |
| PROSPER (2002): age 70–82, with or at risk of vascular disease | 3.2 y | 298/2,891 (10.3%) | 306/2,913 (10.5%) | 2 | HR 0.97 (0.83–1.14) [01.12][01.13] |
| ALLHAT-LLT (2002): age 55+, hypertension, 14% with coronary disease; open label against usual care | 4.8 y | 631/5,170 (12.2%) | 641/5,185 (12.4%) | 2 | RR 0.99 (0.89–1.11) [01.14] |
| ASCOT-LLA (2003): hypertension plus three risk factors | median 3.3 y | 185/5,168 (3.6%) | 212/5,137 (4.1%) | 5–6 | HR 0.87 (0.71–1.06) [01.16][01.42] |
| CARDS (2004): type 2 diabetes | median 3.9 y | 4.3% of 1,428 | 5.8% of 1,410 | about 15 | 27% lower (−1 to 48), P=0.059 [01.19][01.46] |
| MEGA (2006): Japan, 68% women; open label against diet | mean 5.3 y | 2.7 per 1,000 patient-years | 3.8 per 1,000 patient-years | about 6 | HR 0.72, P=0.055 [01.20][01.21][01.42] |
| JUPITER (2008): LDL under 130 mg/dL, raised CRP | median 1.9 y | 2.22% of 8,901 | 2.77% of 8,901 | 5–6 | HR 0.80 (0.67–0.97) [01.22][01.23][01.46] |
| HOPE-3 (2016): intermediate risk, 21 countries | median 5.6 y | 334/6,361 (5.3%) | 357/6,344 (5.6%) | 4 | interval not read [01.25] |
| STAREE (2026): age 70+, no cardiovascular disease or diabetes | median 5.9 y | 13.0 per 1,000 person-years | 14.3 per 1,000 person-years | about 8 | HR 0.91 (0.79–1.04) [01.26][01.27] |

- Only JUPITER reported a statistically significant fall in deaths, and it was stopped at its first interim analysis after a median of 1.9 years [01.22]. The US label for rosuvastatin states that death from cardiovascular causes did not differ significantly between JUPITER's arms (fatal heart attacks 9 on rosuvastatin v 6 on placebo; fatal strokes 3 v 6) [01.23]. The JUPITER and CARDS percentages correspond to about 198 v 247 and 61 v 82 deaths (my arithmetic) [01.46].
- The WOSCOPS totals are my sum of 50 v 73 cardiovascular deaths (US pravastatin label) and 56 v 62 non-cardiovascular deaths (trial paper); they match the 3.2% v 4.1% tabulated elsewhere [01.5][01.8][01.46].
- ALLHAT-LLT, funded under a US National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute contract with additional financial support from Pfizer and pravastatin supplied by Bristol-Myers Squibb, had death from any cause as its primary outcome. It was not blinded, and mean follow-up was 4.8 years. It was resized from 20,000 to about 10,000 people (84% power for a 20% fall), and 29–32% of the usual-care arm started lipid drugs, leaving an LDL difference of 16.7%; the authors say it "lacked the power" to tell the expected benefit from none [01.14].
- In HOPE-3, non-cardiovascular deaths were 180 v 186; by subtraction cardiovascular deaths were 154 v 171 [01.25].

### 3. Pooled analyses, all populations

The Cholesterol Treatment Trialists (CTT) pooled individual records. Their 2005 round (14 trials, 90,056 people, 8,186 deaths) found RR 0.88 (0.84–0.91) per 1.0 mmol/L [01.32]. In 2010, across 26 trials, death fell 10% per 1.0 mmol/L of LDL lowered (RR 0.90, 0.87–0.93): vascular deaths fell 14%, deaths from non-vascular causes did not change (RR 0.97, 0.92–1.03). The 21 statin-versus-control trials held 129,526 people followed for a median 4.8 years, with 12,376 deaths [01.33]. The paper's text does not give deaths by arm, so it cannot be turned into an absolute figure from the text I read. Byrne 2022 (authors report no conflicts) did that conversion from published reports: in 19 trials (66,366 v 66,397 people, average 4.4 years) the absolute fall in deaths was 0.8% (0.4–1.2), that is 8 per 1,000, with a relative fall of 9% (5–14); 0.9% (0.5–2.4) in secondary-prevention trials and 0.6% (0.2–1.0) in primary-prevention trials [01.45].

### 4. Pooled analyses, primary prevention: why they disagree

| Analysis | What was pooled | People with existing disease | Deaths, relative | Deaths, absolute |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brugts 2009 [01.42] | 9 trials with deaths, 67,476 people, mean 4.1 y | about 6% could not be removed | OR 0.88 (0.81–0.96) | 5.1% v 5.7%; 6 per 1,000 |
| Ray 2010 [01.40][01.41] | 11 trials, 65,229 people, 2,793 deaths, mean 3.7 y | removed, using data supplied by trialists | RR 0.91 (0.83–1.01) | not in abstract |
| Therapeutics Initiative 2010 [01.49] | 11 trials, Ray's data | removed | RR 0.93 (0.86–1.00); 0.99 (0.90–1.08) after dropping 4 trials judged at high risk of bias | 0.3%; nil |
| CTT 2012 [01.34] | 27 trials, individual records | removed person by person | RR 0.91 (0.85–0.97) per mmol/L | not given |
| Cochrane 2013 [01.43] | 13 trials with deaths, 48,060 people | trials with up to 10% allowed | OR 0.86 (0.79–0.94) | 4.4% v 5.1%; NNT 96 (64–244) over 5 y |
| USPSTF review 2022 [01.44] | 18 trials, 85,186 people, 1–6 y | primary-prevention data only | RR 0.92 (0.87–0.98) | −0.35% (−0.57 to −0.14); NNT 286 |
| Morsell 2026 [01.53] | statin trials among 10 lipid-drug trials | trials with any such participant excluded | RR 0.96 (0.84–1.10) | 4% v 4% |

The estimates differ for three reasons the papers themselves give. First, who is counted: the two analyses that removed people with existing disease without individual records (Ray, Therapeutics Initiative) have intervals touching or crossing 1.00, and those that left some in show a clearer effect [01.40][01.42][01.49]. Second, early stopping: the Therapeutics Initiative lost the effect when it removed three trials stopped early for benefit and one not analysed by intention to treat, and the USPSTF review found the mortality estimate "no longer statistically significant" when restricted to trials not stopped early or with at least three years of follow-up, with JUPITER the main driver [01.44][01.49]. Third, scaling: CTT weights each trial by its LDL reduction and reports per 1.0 mmol/L, which is a relative figure [01.34]. A search result, not the paper, gives Ray's counts as 1,346/32,623 v 1,447/32,606, about 3 per 1,000; treat this as a lead to check. Morsell 2026 is weaker: it lists ALLHAT-LLT, whose own report says 14% had coronary disease [01.14][01.53].

Across these analyses the absolute fall in deaths in primary prevention is between nil and 8 per 1,000 over roughly four to five years. Yourman 2021 found that one of eight primary-prevention trials in adults aged 50–75 lowered deaths, while 100 people treated for 2.5 years avoided one major cardiovascular event [01.48].

### 5. Baseline risk
CTT 2012 states: "There were too few deaths among the lower risk participants to allow reliable direct assessment of the effects of statin therapy." The relative effect on vascular death looked similar across risk groups (trend P=0.7). For people with under 10% five-year risk of a major vascular event it reports 11 fewer such events per 1,000 over five years per 1.0 mmol/L, and no figure for deaths [01.34]. A fall in deaths has not been shown directly in low-risk people.

### 6. Adults over 70 and over 75
- CTT 2019: 14,483 (8%) of 186,854 participants were over 75. Death fell 9% per mmol/L overall (RR 0.91, 0.88–0.93). The paper concludes there is "less direct evidence of benefit among patients older than 75 years who do not already have evidence of occlusive vascular disease" and that "very large trials … would be needed to provide direct evidence of a mortality reduction among older people in primary prevention" [01.36].
- PROSPER (age 70–82, mixed): cardiovascular deaths 122 v 154 (HR 0.77, 0.61–0.98), non-cardiovascular deaths 176 v 152 (HR 1.18, 0.95–1.46), all deaths 298 v 306. At 8.6 years, 1,229 v 1,234 had died (HR 0.99, 0.91–1.07) [01.13].
- ALLHAT-LLT, post hoc, people 65+ without cardiovascular disease: HR for death 1.18 (0.97–1.42); age 75+: 1.34 (0.98–1.84) [01.15].
- JUPITER, age 70+, exploratory: 1.63 v 2.04 deaths per 100 person-years (HR 0.80, 0.62–1.04) [01.24].
- STAREE (2026; 9,971 Australians aged 70+, mean 74.7, 52% women; atorvastatin 40 mg): major cardiovascular events 297 v 412 (HR 0.70, 0.61–0.82); death, dementia or persistent disability 637 v 676 (HR 0.94, 0.84–1.05) [01.26]. A summary of the paper gives death at 13.0 v 14.3 per 1,000 person-years (HR 0.91, 0.79–1.04) and cardiovascular death at 2.4 v 2.4 (HR 1.00, 0.72–1.37) [01.27]. About 80% of deaths were non-cardiovascular [01.57]. Its target was cut from 18,000 [01.28].
- Two 2026 trials tested stopping statins in older people without cardiovascular disease. SAGA/SITE (France, median age 80, primary outcome death): 7.9% died on continued statin v 7.2% after stopping at three years (difference −0.68%, −3.95 to 2.60), within its 5% margin [01.30]. STREAM (mean age 77.5, two or more chronic conditions): heart attack, ischaemic stroke or death in 12.7% after stopping v 9.2% continuing over a median 103 weeks; event-free time was 2.0 weeks shorter (0.3–3.7) at two years, inside the 5.2-week margin [01.29].
- PREVENTABLE (United States, target 20,000 aged 75+) is listed as recruiting with no results posted; estimated primary completion 31 December 2026 (registry checked 21 November 2026) [01.31].
- Gencer 2020 pooled people 75 and older: cardiovascular death fell (RR 0.85, 0.74–0.98) [01.51]; a critical commentary reports its all-cause figure as RR 0.97 (0.82–1.15) [01.52].

None of the randomised trials found for this thread showed a statistically significant fall in deaths from any cause in people over 70 without cardiovascular disease.

### 7. Women
CTT 2015: 46,675 (27%) of participants were women; death fell per mmol/L in women (RR 0.91, 99% CI 0.84–0.99) and men (0.90, 0.86–0.95), primary and secondary prevention combined [01.35]. In primary-prevention trials alone, 13,154 women had 216 deaths, RR 0.78 (0.53–1.15) [01.50].

### 8. Time gained
Kristensen 2015 measured the area between survival curves in 11 trials: death was postponed by −5 to 19 days in primary-prevention trials (median 3.2) and −10 to 27 days in secondary-prevention trials (median 4.1) over 2.0–6.1 years; 27 days in 4S, 22 in LIPID [01.46]. Hansen 2019, 16 trials: 12.6 days (7.1–18.0); 10.2 primary, 17.4 secondary [01.47]. These are averages over everyone treated, within the trial period only [01.46].

### 9. After the trials ended (observational: both arms were free to take statins)
- 4S, 10.4 years: 414 v 468 dead (RR 0.85, 0.74–0.97) [01.56].
- HPS, 11 years: no further gain after the trial (post-trial deaths 1,962 v 1,949; RR 0.98); the in-trial gap "persisted roughly unchanged" [01.7].
- WOSCOPS, 20 years: HR 0.87 (0.80–0.94) [01.9].
- ASCOT-LLA: at 11 years, UK participants, 460 v 520 dead (HR 0.86, 0.76–0.98), driven by non-cardiovascular deaths [01.17]; at 20 years no significant difference in deaths [01.18].

### 10. Harms that bear on net mortality
Deaths caused by a statin are already inside the all-cause counts above.
- Deaths from non-vascular causes: RR 0.97 (0.92–1.03); cancer incidence RR 1.00 (0.96–1.04) [01.33]. PROSPER's excess of new cancers (HR 1.25, 1.04–1.51) fell to 1.08 (0.96–1.21) on extended follow-up [01.12][01.13].
- Bleeding stroke: 257 v 220 (RR 1.12 per mmol/L, 0.93–1.35); 1.21 (1.05–1.41) once two further trials are added [01.33]. About 0.5 extra per 1,000 over five years per mmol/L [01.34].
- New diabetes: 1.3% v 1.2% a year on low or moderate doses (RR 1.10, 1.04–1.16); 4.8% v 3.5% a year on high doses (RR 1.36, 1.25–1.48), in trials that tested blood sugar often [01.38]. Earlier estimate: one extra case per 255 treated for four years [01.55].
- Muscle: pain or weakness reported by 27.1% v 26.6% over 4.3 years; an excess of 11 per 1,000 person-years in year one and none after [01.37]. Myopathy about 0.5 and rhabdomyolysis about 0.1 extra per 1,000 over five years [01.34].
- Liver test abnormalities: 0.13% a year extra; blinded trials did not support memory loss, depression, sleep disturbance or neuropathy as statin effects [01.39].
- Serious adverse events overall: RR 0.97 (0.93–1.01) [01.44]; 0.99 (0.96–1.03) in the six trials that reported them [01.49].

### 11. Funding, early stopping, run-in periods
- Funders read in the source: CARE, LIPID, WOSCOPS: Bristol-Myers Squibb [01.3][01.4][01.8]. HPS: Medical Research Council, British Heart Foundation, Merck, Roche Vitamins [01.7]. JUPITER: AstraZeneca [01.22]. HOPE-3: Canadian Institutes of Health Research and AstraZeneca [01.25]. ALLHAT-LLT: US National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute contract, financial support from Pfizer, and study drugs from Pfizer, AstraZeneca and Bristol-Myers Squibb, per the paper's funding note [01.14]. STAREE: Australian public and charity funds [01.27]. STREAM: Swiss National Science Foundation [01.29]. SAGA/SITE: French Ministry of Health [01.30]. Every trial in the Cochrane primary-prevention review had full or part industry funding [01.43].
- Stopped early: LIPID (mortality boundary crossed) [01.4]; ASCOT-LLA [01.16]; CARDS, two years early [01.19]; JUPITER [01.22]; AFCAPS/TexCAPS [01.43].
- Run-in before randomisation: LIPID, eight weeks placebo [01.4]; JUPITER, four weeks placebo [01.22]; STAREE, four weeks placebo [01.28]; HOPE-3, four weeks on the active drugs, removing people who did not tolerate or adhere [01.25].
- Designed around deaths: 4S [01.1], HPS [01.6], ALLHAT-LLT [01.14], SAGA/SITE [01.30]. The rest were sized for cardiovascular events.

## What this means for the person's situation
No individual is involved; this is for any adult offered a statin.

- If you already have cardiovascular disease, statin trials show fewer deaths from any cause: about 17 to 33 fewer per 1,000 people over five to six years in 4S, LIPID and HPS, and a smaller, statistically uncertain difference in CARE. These trials are 24 to 32 years old, and other treatments have changed since.
- If you have no cardiovascular disease, the trials show fewer heart attacks and strokes. For deaths, the pooled estimates run from nil to about 8 fewer per 1,000 over four to five years, and the result depends on which trials are counted. Among primary-prevention trials that ran to their planned end, none showed a statistically significant fall in deaths; WOSCOPS came closest (P=0.051).
- If you are over 70 with no cardiovascular disease, no trial has shown fewer deaths. STAREE showed fewer cardiovascular events and no gain in survival free of dementia or disability.
- For women without cardiovascular disease and for people at low risk, a fall in deaths has not been shown directly; there were too few deaths to tell.
- No trial group showed more deaths on a statin. The harms listed are mostly non-fatal.
- Average time gained within a trial was days to weeks. An average hides the split between the few who avoid a fatal event and the many whose date of death does not change.

The starting position, that lowering cholesterol may not be advisable when the measure is death from any cause, is not supported for people with existing disease. For people without it, the trials neither establish nor exclude a small fall in deaths; the decision then rests on non-fatal events against the harms above. Nothing here depends on the assumed country: the trials are international, and only the US review and US drug labels are country-specific. Absolute figures scale with a person's own risk, so they will differ where death rates differ.

## Load-bearing claims
- C01.1: In 4S, 182 of 2,221 people on simvastatin and 256 of 2,223 on placebo died over a median 5.4 years (RR 0.70, 95% CI 0.58–0.85). [01.1][01.2]
- C01.2: In the Heart Protection Study, 1,328 of 10,269 (12.9%) on simvastatin and 1,507 of 10,267 (14.7%) on placebo died (P=0.0003). [01.6]
- C01.3: In ALLHAT-LLT, funded under a US government contract with additional financial support from Pfizer and pravastatin supplied by Bristol-Myers Squibb, and with death from any cause as primary outcome, 631 of 5,170 on pravastatin and 641 of 5,185 on usual care died (RR 0.99, 95% CI 0.89–1.11). [01.14]
- C01.4: The 2022 USPSTF evidence review of primary prevention (18 trials, 85,186 people) found RR 0.92 (0.87–0.98) for death from any cause, an absolute difference of −0.35% (NNT 286) after 1–6 years, not statistically significant when restricted to trials not stopped early. [01.44]
- C01.5: CTT 2012 found RR 0.91 (0.85–0.97) per 1.0 mmol/L for death from any cause in people with no history of vascular disease, and stated there were too few deaths among lower-risk participants for reliable direct assessment. [01.34]
- C01.6: In STAREE (9,971 adults aged 70+, median 5.9 years), death, dementia or persistent disability occurred in 637 on atorvastatin and 676 on placebo (HR 0.94, 0.84–1.05), while major cardiovascular events occurred in 297 and 412 (HR 0.70, 0.61–0.82). [01.26]

## Confidence Notes
- Strong: fewer deaths in people with existing coronary or arterial disease (4S, LIPID, HPS; counts read from abstracts, a full paper and US labels). Strong also that no trial group showed a significant excess of deaths.
- Moderate: the size of any fall in deaths in primary prevention. Seven pooled analyses give point estimates of 0.86 to 0.96; whether the interval excludes 1.00 depends on trial selection and on JUPITER, which stopped early.
- Thin: adults over 75, women in primary prevention, low-risk people. STAREE's death-from-any-cause figures come from a secondary summary of the paper [01.27], not the paper; the abstract gives only the composite. I estimate about 383 v 420 deaths from the published rates; that is my back-calculation and is not in the table.
- "read: full" means I retrieved the full text and read every passage on deaths, design and funding; I did not read each paper end to end. NEJM, JAMA and Lancet sites showed bot checks; I used Internet Archive copies (CARE, LIPID, WOSCOPS, JUPITER, HOPE-3, ALLHAT-LLT), where results tables are images, so some counts come from running text or drug labels. Lancet full texts (4S, HPS, PROSPER, ASCOT-LLA, CARDS, MEGA) were paywalled: abstracts only. MEGA's deaths come from a secondary summary.
- Tiers: CTT individual-record analyses are marked primary because their data exist nowhere else; literature-based meta-analyses and commentaries are secondary. CTT data cannot be re-analysed by outsiders (thread 04). CTT 2012's reply to critics [01.34] and Collins 2016 [01.54] are the main mainstream responses; I read the first in full and the second as an abstract.
- Conflicts: every drug-company trial above was sponsor-funded; Brugts 2009 authors declare industry ties; Byrne 2022, Kristensen 2015 and Kaess declare none; the Therapeutics Initiative is a university group whose letter argues a position.
- Trial figures I remembered were checked against a source; funders of 4S, PROSPER, ASCOT-LLA, CARDS, MEGA and AFCAPS individually were not confirmed in a text I read. HPS's pre-randomisation run-in is not described in the texts I read.
- Crude differences per 1,000 ignore differing follow-up within a trial; ALLHAT-LLT's six-year life-table rates were 14.9% v 15.3% [01.14].
- No page contained instructions addressed to me.

# Cholesterol lowering and death from any cause — Research brief

Confirmed 2026-11-21. This is the second of two linked runs; the first is `thyroid-cholesterol`.

## Question
Does lowering cholesterol reduce death from any cause, and not only cardiovascular events? For whom, by how much in absolute terms, and by which means?

## What this is for
A public evidence review, released as a worked example with the knowledge-superpowers research skill. A reader should be able to use it when weighing a cholesterol-lowering drug, and to check every claim against its source.

## Your situation
- No individual's situation is used. The report is written for a general adult reader and contains no personal information about anyone.
- Country: US guidelines first, with UK and European guidelines for comparison (assumption).
- Starting position supplied with the request. It is to be tested, not assumed: lowering cholesterol may no longer be advisable when the measure is death from any cause, as opposed to fewer cardiovascular events.

## Deliverable
Both: a sourced reference, and a decision section written for any adult who has been offered a cholesterol-lowering drug. For public release. Markdown only.

## Scope
- **In:** adults; statins and every other way of lowering LDL cholesterol (ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors, bempedoic acid, inclisiran, fibrates, niacin, older drugs, diet trials); death from any cause as the main outcome, with cardiovascular death and events alongside; primary and secondary prevention reported separately, and results by age, sex and baseline risk; absolute effects (deaths avoided per 1,000 treated, time gained); harms that bear on net mortality; cohort and genetic evidence on cholesterol level and lifespan; funding, data access and conflicts of interest on both sides of the dispute.
- **Out:** thyroid (the companion run); individual medical advice; children and pregnancy; triglyceride-only treatments except where a trial reports deaths; the wider diet debate beyond trials that report deaths.
- **Time window:** evidence from any year; guidelines, drug approvals and trial results as of November 2026.

## Bias note
Produced by the blind-variables pass on 2026-11-21.

### Information landscape
- **Strong coverage:** statin trials and their pooled analyses; cardiovascular events as the outcome; guideline text.
- **Weak coverage:** death from any cause as a planned outcome; adults over 75, women in primary prevention, and people at low risk; trials longer than about five years; mortality results for the newer drugs.
- **Distorted coverage:** relative-risk headlines without absolute numbers; combined endpoints; trials stopped early; cohort curves quoted without the reverse-causation problem (illness lowers cholesterol); retellings of old diet trials by either camp.

### Key distortions
1. Sponsors fund and run nearly all large lipid-drug trials, which are sized for cardiovascular events and not for deaths. A single trial showing "no mortality benefit" is often too small to show one, and the pooled patient-level data are held by a group that outsiders cannot audit (research economics).
2. Guideline panels and consensus statements share authors with the trials, and their framing (risk reduction per unit of LDL lowered) dominates what gets repeated (institutional amplification).
3. The sceptic side has sellers too: books, diets, supplements and media. It leans on cohort curves and a few re-analyses (research economics).
4. Statins have become a tribal subject. Critics are labelled as spreading misinformation, and some critics allege conspiracy; both habits discourage reading the other side's best work (social filtering).
5. A language model defaults to guideline wording, may misremember trial figures, may blur cardiovascular death with death from any cause, and tends to confirm the framing it is handed (AI filters).

### Compensation strategy
- Take deaths from each trial's own paper: numbers in each arm, absolute difference, length of follow-up, funder, and whether the trial stopped early.
- Report primary and secondary prevention, older adults and women separately, in absolute terms.
- Give weight to publicly funded trials and to analyses by people without industry ties, and record conflicts for key sources on both sides.
- Read the strongest critics and the strongest replies in the original, and say where each is weakest.
- For cohort findings, report how reverse causation was handled, and set genetic evidence beside them.
- Check the current state of trials in older adults and of access to the pooled trial data.

### Confidence calibration
- Expected to be firm: fewer deaths with statins in people who already have cardiovascular disease.
- Uncertain until shown otherwise: any effect on deaths in low-risk primary prevention, in adults over 75, and from the non-statin drugs; what the cohort curves mean.

## Threads
1. `01-statins-all-cause-mortality` — Deaths from any cause in statin trials and pooled analyses, by primary and secondary prevention, age, sex and baseline risk, in absolute terms, with the harms that bear on net mortality. The core evidence.
2. `02-other-lipid-drugs-and-diet-trials` — The same outcome for every other route: ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors, bempedoic acid, inclisiran, fibrates, niacin, older drugs and diet trials. Tests whether lowering cholesterol itself extends life, or only statins do.
3. `03-cohorts-and-genetics` — Cholesterol level and lifespan in long-term cohorts and genetic studies: the U-shaped curves, findings in older people, reverse causation, and people born with very low or very high LDL. The evidence both camps cite outside trials.
4. `04-disputes-and-conflicts` — The strongest sceptic case against cholesterol lowering and the strongest mainstream reply: funding, data access, how benefits are presented, and where each side is weakest. The counter-case thread, run in both directions because the question leans one way.

Every thread also reports what has not been studied and why.

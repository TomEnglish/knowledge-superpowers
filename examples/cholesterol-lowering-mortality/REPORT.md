# Does lowering cholesterol reduce death from any cause? Trials, cohorts and genetic evidence to November 2026

## Research Question

Does lowering cholesterol reduce death from any cause, and not only cardiovascular events? For whom, by how much in absolute terms, and by which means? It covers adults, every way of lowering LDL cholesterol, cohort and genetic evidence, and the dispute over funding and data, as of 21 November 2026. Questions about thyroid function belong to the companion report, *Thyroid function and cholesterol: what the evidence supports before a cholesterol-lowering drug*.

Terms. Death from any cause counts every death; cardiovascular death counts deaths attributed to heart or blood-vessel disease; cardiovascular events are heart attacks, strokes, artery-opening procedures and cardiovascular deaths, usually counted together. Secondary prevention treats people who already have cardiovascular disease, primary prevention people who do not. A hazard ratio (HR) or risk ratio (RR) of 0.80 means a 20% lower rate on treatment; an interval that includes 1.00 means no difference could not be ruled out. "Per 1,000" figures are this report's arithmetic from the counts shown, a crude difference over the trial's follow-up. A "nominal" result is one the trial's pre-set testing order had stopped before reaching, so it is not a formal finding. An LDL change of 1.0 mmol/L is about 39 mg/dL. Where a statement rests only on an abstract, the sentence says so.

## Summary

For people who already have cardiovascular disease, statins reduced death from any cause in the trials designed to test it: in 4S, 182 of 2,221 people on simvastatin and 256 of 2,223 on placebo died from any cause over a median 5.4 years (about 33 fewer per 1,000), and in the Heart Protection Study 12.9% against 14.7% died from any cause (about 18 fewer per 1,000), both from the trial abstracts [01.1][01.2][01.6]. Without cardiovascular disease, statins reduce cardiovascular events but the effect on death from any cause is small and fragile: the US Preventive Services Task Force's review found RR 0.92 (0.87 to 0.98), 3.5 fewer deaths per 1,000 (NNT 286) after 1 to 6 years, not statistically significant once trials stopped early were set aside, and in STAREE, in 9,971 adults aged 70 or over, atorvastatin cut major cardiovascular events (297 against 412) without a significant change in death, dementia or persistent disability (637 against 676; abstract) [01.44][01.26]. No other cholesterol-lowering method has reduced death from any cause as a trial's planned main result: ezetimibe, bempedoic acid and evolocumab in FOURIER left it unchanged, and the two antibody trials with fewer deaths from any cause, ODYSSEY OUTCOMES and VESALIUS-CV (434 of 6,129 on evolocumab against 539 of 6,128 on placebo, HR 0.80, 0.70 to 0.91), reported results that are nominal under their prespecified testing orders [02.1][02.8][02.28][02.12][02.17][02.86]. Cohorts show higher death rates from any cause at low measured cholesterol, but illness lowering cholesterol before death, with confounding, appears to explain most of it, and in genetic studies lifelong lower LDL goes with fewer cardiovascular deaths and no excess of deaths from any cause [03.7][03.8][03.25]. The starting position, that lowering cholesterol may no longer be advisable when judged by death from any cause, failed for statins in people with cardiovascular disease, was not supported for higher-risk people without it, held only in the weak sense of "no demonstrated reduction in deaths" for adults over 70 without cardiovascular disease and most non-statin drugs, and could not be tested in low-risk people or women without cardiovascular disease for lack of deaths; no trial was large enough to show that lowering LDL fails to reduce deaths.

## What this means for you

This section is for any adult who has been offered a cholesterol-lowering drug. It gives evidence and questions to take to a doctor, grouped by the situations the trials separate.

It is not individual medical advice, and nothing in it is a reason to start, stop or change a medicine.

- **If you already have cardiovascular disease** (a heart attack, angina, a stroke from a blocked artery, or narrowed arteries elsewhere). The evidence that statins reduce death from any cause is firmest here. In 4S, people with coronary heart disease had about 33 fewer deaths from any cause per 1,000 over a median 5.4 years on simvastatin [01.1][01.2]. In the Heart Protection Study (coronary or other arterial disease, or diabetes), over a scheduled five years about 18 fewer per 1,000 died from any cause and about 54 fewer per 1,000 had a major vascular event (abstract) [01.6]. In both trials the fall came from fewer coronary and vascular deaths [01.1][01.6]. The trials date from 1994 and 2002, and two statin trials in heart failure found no change in deaths from any cause [01.46]. Ask: what is my chance of dying from any cause, and separately of a heart attack or stroke, over five years with and without this drug?

- **If you have no cardiovascular disease and your risk is raised** (for example diabetes, or several risk factors together). Statins reduce heart attacks and strokes; the fall in death from any cause is small. The 2022 USPSTF evidence review of primary prevention (18 trials, 85,186 people) found RR 0.92 (0.87 to 0.98) for death from any cause, an absolute difference of 0.35 percentage points (NNT 286) after 1 to 6 years, not statistically significant when restricted to trials not stopped early [01.44]. A 2022 analysis of six primary-prevention trials found 6 fewer deaths from any cause per 1,000 (95% CI 2 to 10), in an analysis whose trials averaged 4.4 years [01.45]. Ask for the absolute reduction at your risk, separately for death from any cause and for heart attacks and strokes, and which trials it comes from.

- **If you have no cardiovascular disease and your risk is low.** A fall in death from any cause has not been demonstrated, which is not the same as shown not to exist: the Cholesterol Treatment Trialists (CTT) stated there were too few deaths among lower-risk participants for reliable direct assessment [01.34]. What has been shown is fewer cardiovascular events: at under 10% five-year risk, about 11 fewer major vascular events (heart attacks, coronary deaths, strokes and coronary procedures together) per 1,000 over five years per 1.0 mmol/L of LDL lowering [01.34]. Women without cardiovascular disease are in a similar position: their primary-prevention trials had too few deaths to show an effect either way (abstract) [01.50]. The choice then rests on fewer non-fatal events against the harms below.

- **If you are over 70, or over 75, without cardiovascular disease.** In STAREE (median 5.9 years), major cardiovascular events occurred in 297 on atorvastatin and 412 on placebo (HR 0.70, 0.61 to 0.82), while death, dementia or persistent disability occurred in 637 and 676 (HR 0.94, 0.84 to 1.05), both from the abstract [01.26]. A secondary summary gives arms of 4,984 and 4,987, so about 23 fewer people per 1,000 had a cardiovascular event, and gives death from any cause as 13.0 against 14.3 per 1,000 person-years (HR 0.91, 0.79 to 1.04) and cardiovascular death as 2.4 against 2.4 [01.27]. No randomised trial found here showed a significant fall in deaths from any cause in this group, and CTT says the direct evidence of benefit over 75 without vascular disease is thinner [01.36]. PREVENTABLE (target 20,000 people aged 75 or over; listed primary outcomes freedom from new dementia and from persistent disability at four years) had posted no results on 21 November 2026, with primary completion estimated for 31 December 2026 [01.31]. Two 2026 trials of stopping statins are described below as evidence to discuss with a doctor, not a reason to stop [01.29][01.30]. Ask which matters more to you, fewer heart attacks and strokes or longer life.

- **If you are offered a drug other than a statin.** Death from any cause was unchanged with ezetimibe added to a statin after an acute coronary syndrome (1,215 of 9,067 against 1,231 of 9,077 over a median 6 years; HR 0.99, 0.91 to 1.07) and with evolocumab in established cardiovascular disease (444 of 13,784 against 426 of 13,780 over a median 2.2 years; HR 1.04, 0.91 to 1.19) [02.1][02.8][02.9]. VESALIUS-CV enrolled people under 80 with significant coronary, cerebrovascular or peripheral artery disease or high-risk diabetes, raised LDL, non-HDL cholesterol or apolipoprotein B, and no previous heart attack or stroke [02.17]; 434 of 6,129 on evolocumab and 539 of 6,128 on placebo died from any cause over a median 4.6 years (HR 0.80, 0.70 to 0.91), about 17 fewer per 1,000, a result that is nominal under the trial's prespecified testing order, as the US label states [02.17][02.86]. Alirocumab after an acute coronary syndrome gave 334 of 9,462 against 392 of 9,462 deaths from any cause over a median 2.8 years (HR 0.85, 0.73 to 0.98), about 6 fewer per 1,000, also nominal [02.11][02.12]. Bempedoic acid in statin-intolerant people left deaths from any cause unchanged (434 of 6,992 against 420 of 6,978; HR 1.03, 0.90 to 1.18; median 40.6 months) [02.28]. Inclisiran had no outcome-trial result by 21 November 2026 [02.24][02.25]. Fibrates and niacin did not reduce deaths from any cause, and niacin with laropiprant had 798 deaths among 12,838 against 732 among 12,835 on placebo over a median 3.9 years, about 5 more per 1,000 [02.54]. Ask whether the drug was tested in people like you, and whether it changed death from any cause or only cardiovascular events.

- **Harms that bear on staying alive.** Deaths a drug causes are already inside the counts of deaths from any cause. In 19 placebo-controlled trials (123,940 participants), 27.1% allocated a statin and 26.6% allocated placebo reported muscle pain or weakness, an excess of 11 (6 to 16) per 1,000 person-years in year 1 [04.4]. New diabetes ran at 1.3% against 1.2% a year on low- or moderate-intensity statins and 4.8% against 3.5% on high-intensity statins, the absolute excess depending mainly on how often trials tested blood sugar [01.38]. Bleeding strokes may rise by about 0.5 per 1,000 over five years per 1.0 mmol/L of LDL lowering, by CTT's estimate [01.34].

- **What would change this.** Results from PREVENTABLE and the inclisiran outcome trials; the full VESALIUS-CV papers and an analysis by anyone outside the sponsor; independent access to the pooled statin data, which CTT does not release [01.31][02.24][04.2]. So would facts a general report cannot know: your disease history, estimated risk, LDL level and other illnesses.

- **Assumptions.** The brief assumed a US reader. The trials are international; only the USPSTF review and the US labels are country-specific [01.44][02.86]. The research did not cover what US, UK or European guidelines now recommend, so this report does not say.

Three of the four trials reduced death from any cause, each through fewer cardiovascular deaths: 4S had 46 against 49 non-cardiovascular deaths, HPS 547 against 570 non-vascular deaths, and LIPID 331 against 433 cardiovascular deaths [01.1][01.6][01.5]. HPS carries particular weight, because death from any cause was a primary outcome and its funders included the Medical Research Council and British Heart Foundation as well as Merck and Roche Vitamins [01.6][01.7]. CARE was sized for coronary events, not deaths, and LIPID stopped early when its mortality boundary was crossed [01.3][01.4]. In heart failure, statins did not lower deaths from any cause: 28.8% against 28.1% died in GISSI-HF and 29.0% against 30.4% in CORONA [01.46].

Pooled, CTT found in 2010 that across 26 trials death from any cause fell 10% per 1.0 mmol/L of LDL lowered (RR 0.90, 0.87 to 0.93), with vascular deaths 14% lower and non-vascular deaths unchanged (RR 0.97, 0.92 to 1.03) [01.33]. A 2022 analysis of five secondary-prevention trials gave 9 fewer deaths from any cause per 1,000, though its relative estimate (14% lower, interval −2% to 27%) crossed no effect [01.45].

## People without cardiovascular disease

Only one primary-prevention statin trial showed a statistically significant fall in death from any cause. JUPITER recorded 2.22% against 2.77% of 8,901 per arm dying (HR 0.80, 0.67 to 0.97) and was stopped at its first interim analysis after a median 1.9 years; the US label states that cardiovascular death did not differ significantly [01.22][01.23][01.46]. WOSCOPS came closest among the rest (106 of 3,302 against 135 of 3,293 deaths from any cause, P=0.051) [01.8][01.5]. Deaths from any cause were 80 against 77 in AFCAPS/TexCAPS, 185 against 212 in ASCOT-LLA and 334 against 357 in HOPE-3 [01.11][01.16][01.25]. In ALLHAT-LLT, funded under a US government contract with additional financial support from Pfizer and pravastatin supplied by Bristol-Myers Squibb, and with death from any cause as primary outcome, 631 of 5,170 on pravastatin and 641 of 5,185 on usual care died (RR 0.99, 95% CI 0.89–1.11) [01.14]. It was open-label, 14% of participants had coronary disease, 29% to 32% of the usual-care arm started lipid drugs, and its authors say it lacked the power to tell the expected benefit from none [01.14].

The two analyses that removed people with existing disease without individual records have intervals touching or crossing 1.00 [01.40][01.49]. Removing trials stopped early removes statistical significance: the USPSTF review found the estimate no longer significant in trials not stopped early or with at least three years of follow-up, JUPITER being the main driver, and the Therapeutics Initiative lost the effect in the same way [01.44][01.49]. CTT 2012 found RR 0.91 (0.85–0.97) per 1.0 mmol/L for death from any cause in people with no history of vascular disease, and stated there were too few deaths among lower-risk participants for reliable direct assessment [01.34]. Across the table the absolute fall in deaths from any cause runs from nil to about 7 per 1,000 over four to five years.

Women were 27% of CTT participants, and death from any cause fell in women (RR 0.91, 99% CI 0.84 to 0.99) as in men, primary and secondary prevention combined (abstract) [01.35]. In primary-prevention trials alone, 13,154 women had 216 deaths, RR 0.78 (0.53 to 1.15) (abstract) [01.50].

## Adults over 70 and over 75

Only 8% of participants in CTT's 2019 analysis were over 75; the collaboration concluded that very large trials would be needed to show fewer deaths in older people in primary prevention [01.36]. No separate randomised estimate of deaths from any cause over 75 with existing disease was found.

PROSPER (ages 70 to 82) had fewer cardiovascular deaths on pravastatin (122 against 154, HR 0.77, 0.61 to 0.98) but more non-cardiovascular deaths (176 against 152, HR 1.18, 0.95 to 1.46), so deaths from any cause were 298 against 306, and 1,229 against 1,234 at 8.6 years [01.12][01.13]. A post hoc ALLHAT-LLT analysis of people 65 or over without cardiovascular disease gave HR 1.18 (0.97 to 1.42) for death from any cause, and 1.34 (0.98 to 1.84) at 75 or over (abstract) [01.15]. A 2020 pooled analysis of people 75 or over found fewer cardiovascular deaths (RR 0.85, 0.74 to 0.98; abstract), and a commentary on it reports RR 0.97 (0.82 to 1.15) for death from any cause [01.51][01.52].

In STAREE (9,971 adults aged 70+, median 5.9 years), death, dementia or persistent disability occurred in 637 on atorvastatin and 676 on placebo (HR 0.94, 0.84–1.05), while major cardiovascular events occurred in 297 and 412 (HR 0.70, 0.61–0.82) [01.26]. These come from the abstract; the death-from-any-cause figures come from a secondary summary, and no source read gives deaths by arm [01.27]. About 80% of its deaths were non-cardiovascular, a news report says, which helps explain why fewer cardiovascular events moved deaths from any cause so little [01.57]. It was publicly and charity funded, and its target was cut from 18,000 [01.27][01.28].

Two 2026 trials tested stopping statins in older people without cardiovascular disease, both read as abstracts. In SAGA/SITE (median age 80, primary outcome death from any cause), 7.9% died on continued statins against 7.2% after stopping over three years, within its non-inferiority margin [01.30]. In STREAM (mean age 77.5), heart attack, ischaemic stroke or death occurred in 12.7% after stopping against 9.2% when continuing over a median 103 weeks; event-free time at two years was 2.0 weeks shorter, inside its 5.2-week margin [01.29]. For ezetimibe, EWTOPIA 75 (ages 75 and over, open-label, closed early) recorded 188 of 1,716 against 173 of 1,695 deaths from any cause (HR 1.09, 0.89 to 1.34) [02.5].

In VESALIUS-CV, 434 of 6,129 people on evolocumab and 539 of 6,128 on placebo died (from any cause) over a median 4.6 years (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.70 to 0.91); under the trial's prespecified testing order this result is nominal [02.16][02.17][02.86]. The analysis plan places death from any cause ninth of ten secondary endpoints, after coronary death, which was not significantly reduced (105 against 117, HR 0.89, 0.68 to 1.16) [02.17]. The mortality paper's abstract gives 156 against 195 cardiovascular deaths, and its authors state the trial was not designed or powered for mortality; the sponsor ran it, nobody outside has re-analysed it, and its full papers were not read for this review [02.16][02.17]. ODYSSEY's result is nominal for the same reason: death from any cause sat last in its hierarchy, after coronary and cardiovascular death, neither significant (cardiovascular death 240 against 271, HR 0.88, 0.74 to 1.05) [02.12]. FOURIER, with 870 deaths, could not detect a modest effect either way; a 2022 re-analysis of its regulatory report found the same totals of deaths from any cause but re-classified causes, and the investigators disputed its method without contesting the totals [02.9][04.11].

Some agents raised deaths from any cause through effects other than LDL lowering. In ILLUMINATE, 93 people on torcetrapib and 59 on atorvastatin alone had died when the trial was stopped on 2 December 2006 (HR 1.58, 95% CI 1.14 to 2.19); torcetrapib also raised blood pressure [02.30]. The WHO clofibrate trial found significantly higher total mortality (abstracts), and the Coronary Drug Project stopped its dextrothyroxine arm in 1971 for more deaths than placebo [02.48][02.50][02.59]. Pooled fibrate trials gave RR 1.01 for death from any cause in primary and 0.98 in secondary prevention, and niacin 1.05 (Cochrane abstracts) [02.51][02.52][02.55].

The 2020 Cochrane review of saturated-fat reduction found a risk ratio for death from any cause of 0.96 (95% CI 0.90 to 1.03) across 11 trials with 55,858 participants, against 17% fewer combined cardiovascular events [02.76]. In the Sydney Diet Heart Study 39 of 221 on the diet and 28 of 237 controls died (HR 1.62, 1.00 to 2.64) [02.70]; recovered Minnesota data linked larger cholesterol falls to higher mortality, but that comparison is observational within the trial and, in this review's reading, open to reverse causation [02.69].

Across methods, the same LDL lowering gave similar falls in cardiovascular events (abstract) [02.78], but death from any cause fell with more intensive lowering only where baseline LDL was 100 mg/dL or more (abstract), and larger percentage falls brought no further reduction [02.79][02.80]. The pattern fits a narrower statement than "lowering LDL by any means extends life": fewer deaths from any cause appear where vascular deaths are a large share of all deaths, LDL starts high, the fall is large and sustained, and the agent has no offsetting toxicity. Trial size explains much of the rest. By this report's arithmetic about 2,800 deaths are needed for 80% power to detect a 10% fall in death from any cause, and 630 for 20%; FOURIER, CLEAR Outcomes and ODYSSEY had 726 to 870 deaths each [02.8][02.28][02.11].

## Time gained, long-term follow-up and harms

Within trials, the average gain is measured in days. Across 11 statin trials of 2.0 to 6.1 years, death from any cause was postponed by a median 3.2 days in primary and 4.1 days in secondary prevention [01.46]; a later analysis of 16 trials gave 12.6 days (abstract) [01.47]. A lifetime model for a 50-year-old man at average UK risk, assuming 30% fewer cardiovascular deaths, gives a mean gain of 7 months, with 93% gaining nothing and 7% gaining a mean of 99 months (abstract) [04.15]. After the trials ended both arms could take statins, so later follow-up is observational: 4S at 10.4 years had 414 against 468 deaths from any cause and WOSCOPS at 20 years HR 0.87 (abstracts), while HPS at 11 years showed the in-trial gap persisting without further gain [01.56][01.9][01.7].

The harms that could offset a fall in deaths are small in the trials. Non-vascular deaths (RR 0.97, 0.92 to 1.03) and cancer incidence (RR 1.00) were unchanged [01.33]. Bleeding strokes rose (RR 1.21, 1.05 to 1.41 with two further trials added), myopathy added about 0.5 and rhabdomyolysis about 0.1 cases per 1,000 over five years, and new diabetes rose as described above [01.33][01.34][01.38]. Serious adverse events overall showed RR 0.97 (0.93 to 1.01) [01.44]. No statin trial group showed a significant excess of deaths from any cause [01.33][01.44].

## What the cohort curves and genetic studies add

The curve sceptics cite is real. In 108,243 Danish adults, all-cause mortality was lowest at LDL 3.6 mmol/L (140 mg/dL); the hazard ratio below 1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL) against 3.4 to 3.9 mmol/L was 1.25 (95% CI 1.15 to 1.36), and the U-shape remained, slightly reduced, when follow-up started at year 5 and people with cardiovascular disease, cancer or COPD at baseline were excluded [03.1]. Korean, US national survey and Pittsburgh cohorts show the same U-shape for death from any cause [03.3][03.5][03.6], yet lower total cholesterol went with lower ischaemic heart disease mortality at every age (abstract) [03.16].

The low-cholesterol groups are not random samples. In Copenhagen 47% of the lowest LDL band were on lipid-lowering treatment, 28% had cardiovascular disease and 19% diabetes, against 12%, 9% and 4% overall [03.1]. Illness lowers cholesterol before death. In 99,758 UK adults aged 80 to 105, total cholesterol declined faster in the last 2 years of life; in the final quarter of follow-up the odds of a value below 3.0 mmol/L were 3.33 (2.84 to 3.91) times higher in untreated people who died than in survivors [03.8]. In 12,334 adults aged 65 or over not on lipid drugs (ASPREE), the inverse association between LDL and all-cause mortality disappeared when deaths in the first 5 years were excluded: hazard ratio 1.01 (0.90 to 1.13) per 1 mmol/L higher LDL [03.7]. Reverse causation has not been shown to explain everything: the Copenhagen curve survived its five-year lag, and its authors cannot tell whether the remainder is residual confounding; they also state that their data should not be used against lipid-lowering treatment [03.1]. A cohort curve shows who dies sooner at a given level, not what lowering that level with a drug does.

The 2016 review that found LDL inversely associated with death from any cause in 16 of 30 cohorts of people aged 60 or over counted cohorts by direction without pooling, drew about two-thirds of its participants (by this review's count) from one register study that did not adjust for smoking, weight or illness, and was scored at two of 11 quality criteria by an Oxford appraisal [03.18][03.19][03.20]. It shows that an inverse association is common in older cohorts, not why.

Genetic studies remove reverse causation and most confounding. In a Mendelian randomisation study of 1,012,240 parental lifespans, 1 standard deviation higher genetically predicted LDL cholesterol was associated with 1.2 years shorter lifespan (95% CI 0.87 to 1.55) [03.22]. With Copenhagen (109,566 participants) and UK Biobank combined, each 0.5 mmol/L lower LDL from PCSK9 variants was associated with a risk ratio of 0.79 (0.65 to 0.95) for cardiovascular death and 1.01 (0.94 to 1.08) for death from any cause; Copenhagen alone gave 0.79 (0.63 to 0.99) and 1.02 (0.94 to 1.12) [03.25]. Analyses of UK Biobank participants' own deaths disagree on LDL, but none shows lifelong lower LDL going with more deaths from any cause [03.26][03.27][03.28]. The variants act from conception, not from a drug started in adult life, and the cohorts are almost all of European ancestry [03.27][03.26].

People born with very high LDL show what it does with and without treatment. In the UK Simon Broome register of familial hypercholesterolaemia (3,382 patients, 370 deaths), the all-cause standardised mortality ratio was 1.37 (1.03 to 1.78) in 1980 to 1991 and 0.94 (0.84 to 1.05) from 1992; the coronary ratio fell from 3.39 to 2.13 [03.36]. That change came as statins arrived, but the comparison is not randomised [03.36].

## The dispute: funding, data access and presentation

The dispute runs together arguments about the numbers, their presentation and motive. On the numbers the sides are closer than their language suggests. A 2022 meta-analysis of 21 statin trials found, across the 19 that reported all-cause mortality, an absolute reduction of 0.8% (95% CI 0.4% to 1.2%) and a relative reduction of 9% (95% CI 5% to 14%); its authors include the author of a 2018 reappraisal of the lipid hypothesis [04.12][04.17].

Every trial in the Cochrane primary-prevention review had full or part industry funding [01.43]. Industry-sponsored studies more often report favourable results (abstract), though LDL lowering did not differ between sponsored and other statin trials, which answers the charge for a laboratory measure but not for how deaths are classified (abstract) [04.31][04.33]. The CTT's data-sharing statement in its Lancet paper of 14 February 2026 says individual participant data "would not be released to others" and that requests should be made directly to each trial's data custodians [04.1][04.2]. Outsiders may propose analyses for the CTT to run, an oversight panel has met since 2017, and critics have obtained trial documents from regulators instead, but no re-analysis of the pooled data by an independent group was found [04.2][04.3][04.27][04.48].

On presentation, critics hold that death from any cause is the ultimate measure [04.16]; the mainstream reply treats it as a composite in which statins' effect reflects fewer vascular deaths, and gives absolute figures mainly for vascular events [04.5]. The complaint that stands is that the headline figure counts events, not deaths. The critics' weakest point is counting trials: a table of 29 trials reported after 2005 scored non-significant results as negative and listed JUPITER as showing no mortality benefit, though its abstract gives HR 0.80 (0.67 to 0.97) for death from any cause, and statisticians answering related work note that non-significant is not negative [04.17][04.24][04.22]. The journal editors' rule announced in September 2004 required prospective trial registration for trials starting enrolment after 1 July 2005; it did not require release of trial data, on which the statement is silent [04.20]. Large US government-funded trials did turn null more often after 2000, and prior registration went with null results (abstract) [04.21].

The BMJ's independent panel (report of 15 August 2014) unanimously found that neither 2013 article met the criteria for retraction, and that the only unequivocal error was the 18 to 20% side-effect figure, corrected on 15 May 2014 [04.6]. It also strongly recommended releasing patient-level data, and did not rule on whether statins help low-risk people [04.6]. Conflicts run both ways: 7 of 16 members of the 2013 US guideline panel had industry ties by a count from its own disclosures (abstract), and many CTT collaborators declare fees from lipid-drug makers [04.35][04.1]; authors of the main sceptic reviews declare books critical of the cholesterol hypothesis, and one review was funded by an institute where two of its authors work [04.18][03.18]. This report's reading is that industry money is larger and nearer the primary data, and book and media income smaller and nearer public messaging.

In June 2024 the High Court of England and Wales ruled on preliminary issues in a libel claim by two UK statin critics against a national newspaper. It held that the articles meant the two had knowingly made false public statements about statins, dismissed the newspaper's public-interest defence, found that the then health secretary's statement had been used in a seriously misleading way, and rejected the critics' allegation of malice against the journalist; it said expressly that it was not deciding who is right in the statin debate, and that its judgment did not finally resolve the claim [04.43]. The claim settled in December 2024 with an apology and damages, before the newspaper's remaining defences were tried [04.43][04.44].

After UK media coverage in October 2013 to March 2014, the odds ratio for stopping a statin was 1.11 (primary prevention) and 1.12 (secondary prevention), an effect no longer observed after six months, in a post hoc analysis [04.39]. No study measured deaths caused by coverage [04.39].

## What the evidence did to the starting position

The starting position, to be tested rather than assumed, was that lowering cholesterol may no longer be advisable when the measure is death from any cause.

Two readings are kept apart: no demonstrated reduction in deaths, where trials could not show one, and shown not to reduce deaths, where a trial could rule out a worthwhile reduction.

For statins in people with cardiovascular disease, the position failed: 4S, LIPID and HPS showed fewer deaths from any cause through fewer cardiovascular deaths, and the pooled trial data agree [01.1][01.4][01.6][01.33]. In heart failure it held as no demonstrated reduction [01.46]. For ezetimibe added to a statin it held as no demonstrated reduction, and came nearest to the strong form, since IMPROVE-IT's interval excludes a fall in deaths larger than 9% over six years [02.1]. For PCSK9 antibodies it was weakened but not overturned, because the reductions in ODYSSEY and VESALIUS-CV are nominal and FOURIER was too short to tell [02.12][02.17][02.86][02.8].

For higher-risk people without cardiovascular disease, the position is not supported as stated: every pooled point estimate favours fewer deaths from any cause, by up to about 7 per 1,000 over four to five years, but statistical significance depends on trials stopped early, so the fall is small and fragile rather than absent [01.44][01.49][01.43]. For low-risk people and for women without cardiovascular disease it could not be tested, for lack of deaths [01.34][01.50]. For adults over 70 without cardiovascular disease it held as no demonstrated reduction: STAREE's hazard ratio of 0.91 (0.79 to 1.04) for death from any cause, from a secondary summary, is compatible with 21% fewer or 4% more deaths, beside a clear fall in cardiovascular events, and PREVENTABLE has not reported [01.26][01.27][01.31].

For other means it held as no demonstrated reduction for bempedoic acid, fibrates, niacin and diet, and in the strong form, with more deaths, for torcetrapib, clofibrate and dextrothyroxine [02.28][02.51][02.76][02.30][02.48][02.59]. Those excess deaths are attributed to the agents' other actions, not to lower LDL, which from PCSK9 variants goes with no excess of deaths from any cause [02.30][03.25]. The cohort observation behind the position, higher death rates at low cholesterol, is real but largely explained by illness lowering cholesterol, and cannot speak to treatment [03.1][03.7][03.8].

## What the evidence doesn't say

The trials cannot say what statins do to death from any cause in low-risk people, in women without cardiovascular disease, or in adults over 75, because none was sized for those deaths [01.34][01.36][01.50]. No placebo-controlled trial runs much beyond six years, and lifetime gain is only modelled [01.7][04.15]. This review did not find STAREE's deaths by arm, HOPE-3's hazard ratio for death or the full VESALIUS-CV papers, and no outcome trial of inclisiran or obicetrapib had reported [01.27][01.25][02.17][02.24][02.37]. The genetic evidence is almost entirely European [03.26]. The research did not cover current guideline recommendations, and conflicts of interest were checked only for US guideline panels [04.36][04.37].

The bias note predicted that sponsors would dominate, that trials would be sized for events rather than deaths, and that the pooled data would be closed; all three held [01.43][04.2]. Deaths were taken from each trial's own report, registry record or US label. The trials paid for mainly from public money gave no significant fall in deaths from any cause (ALLHAT-LLT, STAREE) or found stopping non-inferior (SAGA/SITE), while the clearest reduction, in HPS, came from a trial with public and industry funding and death from any cause as a primary outcome [01.14][01.27][01.30][01.6][01.7]. The critics' best work was read in the original and its weakest points named, and each cohort is reported with how it handled reverse causation [04.12][04.17][02.9][03.18][03.1].

Two limits remain. Of 235 sources, 131 were read as abstracts and 8 as snippets, and several key trial counts (4S, HPS, STAREE) rest on abstracts because journal sites returned bot checks that were not worked around [01.1][01.6][01.26]. Nobody outside the trialists can re-analyse the pooled statin data, so fine detail such as exact side-effect rates and small subgroups cannot be audited, though that is not evidence that the main results are wrong [04.1][04.2].
